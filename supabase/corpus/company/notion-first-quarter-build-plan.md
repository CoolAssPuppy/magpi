finishes. This page is the volume and the calendar. It exists because five of us
have asked me some version of "how many are we making" in the last fortnight and
I have given five slightly different answers.

## Why four finishes and not six

Because six do not fit through the line, and I want that written somewhere
people outside supply chain can read it.

Every finish needs its own coating run at the film vendor and its own pass on
the anodising line at the shell vendor. Rust needed two anodising passes rather
than one, and a second pass takes a slot exactly like a first one. There are
nine passes available in the window. Four finishes need eight. Six finishes
needed eleven, and cutting Tide alone would still have left ten.

Tide came out for a different reason. The film vendor's hard coat and
anti-fingerprint stack covers the whole outer face and the rear cover, not only
the display, so it goes over the frame colour everywhere. The stack has a warm
cast that no observer can find on a black, a white, a green or a copper, and
that is the entire shade on a pale blue. Tide failed its colour gate against its
own master swatch and there was nothing to fix, because the cast comes from the
anti-fingerprint chemistry rather than from a process.

Neither cut was about taste. Both trace back to the outer layer reversal on 27
August, and anyone who is asked why the phone comes in four colours can say
that.

Run 5 is filled to the cap rather than to the forecast, which puts 603 more
Ember devices into the quarter than the plan calls for. Ember is one run and
only one, so if Ember demand comes in ahead of the forecast the next slot is six
weeks away. Those 603 are the only buffer that finish has.

Moss is the opposite. Two runs cover 40,195 devices against 50,000 of capacity,
so if the mix moves there is room in the Moss slots and almost none in the Chalk
ones.

## France and Australia are the same date and not the same launch

Tier 2 opens on one date in four markets, six weeks after tier 1, which puts it
in the middle of December.

France opens at the very end of a gifting season. Stock that is not on a shelf
on day one is stranded until spring, so all 9,500 French units have to be in the
European hub before tier 2 opens.

Australia opens at the front of summer and its biggest retail run of the year,
which goes through Boxing Day and into January. Australian demand is
back-weighted, and the 8,000 units still have to be built in the same pre-launch
window, because a January coating run delivers in February. What changes is not
when we make them but how long they sit in Sydney.

So France is served first and served complete, and Australia can wait. I would
rather short Sydney by a week than short Rotterdam by a box.

## What can still move it

**The board to panel 4 flex connector.** Single source, nine weeks quoted,
distributor showing fourteen. First article does not need it because I have
parts in hand. The full run does. I will know by 18 September.

**Lamination yield on the first article.** Nobody has run this bond on four
panels. A bad first article does not stop the plan, it changes how many devices
come out of each run, and I would rather find that in October than in December.

**The mix.** The four-way split above came from research on renders, not from
anyone buying anything. Tier 2 is 31,500 units and it gets built after tier 1
has been on sale for three weeks, so we get one chance to re-cut the colour mix
on real orders. That chance reaches 17.5 percent of the quarter. The other 82.5
percent is committed on the forecast, which is why I keep asking marketing how
much they believe it.
