## Session five. Getting to a pilot line

I ran this one, so these notes are closer to a plan than to minutes.

Where the pilot line stands: a partner in Shenzhen, a slot held, a tooling set
that exists and a second one being cut. The slot is held against a parts
arrival date. Every week the parts date moves, the slot moves with it, and the
slot is shared with three other companies who are also moving.

What the room needed to understand, and I think half of it now does:

1. A supplier saying yes on a call is not a commitment. A purchase order with a
   date and a penalty is a commitment.
2. Long lead parts are long lead whether or not we have decided what they are.
   The hinge steel is sixteen weeks. It does not care that we are still arguing
   about the outer layer.
3. Changing a material changes the qualification queue at the factory, and the
   qualification queue is first come, first served.

Sam asked what would happen if the outer layer changed after the tooling was
cut. I said it depends entirely on whether the change touches the lamination
step. He said it would. I said then it costs weeks and I could not tell him how
many without a specific material in front of me.

That conversation was seven days before it stopped being hypothetical.

## Session six. Suppliers, named and unnamed

We have single-source exposure on three items: the hinge steel, the outer layer
laminate and the panel driver.

The hinge steel is the one that keeps me up. One mill, one specification, one
lead time, and a quality process I have seen once, in person, for two hours.
Jane asked whether we should qualify a second source. The honest answer is that
qualifying a second source takes longer than the time we have before the pilot
line, so the plan is to carry the risk and be loud about carrying it.

Action: I write a one page exposure note per single-source part and put it
where the people who need it can find it. Owner me, done by end of month.

## Session seven. How we decide things

This came out of a complaint and turned into the most useful hour of the two
days.

The complaint, from Priya: the same decision has three names. What Notion calls
the outer layer decision, Linear calls ENG-212, and everybody in Slack calls
the crease thing. She has been in three conversations that were the same
conversation and did not know it.

What we agreed:

- A decision gets written down in one place on the day it is made, with the
  date, the person who made it, and what would have to be true to reverse it.
- The reversal condition is the part we keep skipping and it is the part that
  matters.
- Whoever runs a meeting where a decision is made owns writing it down. Not the
  most junior person in the room. Not nobody.

Jane added the rule that a decision written down after the fact, from memory,
is a story about a decision.

them, which Sam said out loud, and Jane laughed and moved on.
