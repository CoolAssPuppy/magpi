## What we are shipping

Two accessories. That is the whole list and it is deliberately short.

| Accessory | Finishes | Owner, hardware | Owner, go to market |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Folio case | Ink, Chalk, Moss, Ember | Sam | Priya |
| Desk stand | Chalk only | Sam and Ben | Priya |

The folio case comes in all four shipping finishes because a case is worn with
the phone and a mismatch reads as an accident. The desk stand is Chalk only
because a stand sits on a desk, not on the phone, and matching it to four
finishes would mean four tools, four colour approvals and four stock lines for
an object nobody looks at while it is doing its job.

## Why only two

We are seven people. Every accessory is a tool, a supplier, a drop programme, a
box, a stock line and a support burden that lasts as long as the phone does.

The list of things we said no to, so nobody has to ask again: a screen
protector, a charging pad, a keyboard cover, a stylus, a second stand in a
different angle, and a strap.

Two of those deserve a note rather than a line.

**The screen protector.** Meniscus-C is polymer and it scratches more than the
glass we reversed away from. That is exactly the argument for selling a
protector and exactly the reason we are not. A film over the outer layer changes
what the device feels like to fold and adds a surface that can crease
independently. If the outer layer needs protecting, that is a product problem
and not a shopping opportunity.

**The stylus.** Every folding device demo eventually reaches for one. Ori does
not have the input model for it, the panels do not have a digitiser, and adding
either is a next product conversation.

## The desk stand is a software product

Worth saying plainly because it keeps surprising people who see it on a bill of
materials as a moulded part.

You set the phone into the stand, all four panels open, and the shell notices
and lays out for the desk state on its own. No button, no setting. That is the
accessory. The plastic is the delivery mechanism.

Ben has the detection work open and the gate is deliberately fussy. Hinge
angles flat, a magnet with the right polarity, a stable 12 degree tilt held for
a moment, and nothing touching the back. All four have to agree, because a
phone lying flat next to a shut laptop can look like a stand to a single
sensor, and a device that rearranges itself on a table for no reason is worse
than one that never does anything clever at all.

Sam has the mechanical side, and the thing he cares most about is that the
stand touches only the frame. The desk state is our hottest state and the rear
faces of panels 2 and 3 are how we get rid of that heat. A cradle would have
been prettier and would have made the thermal work harder.

## The folio case is the harder object

The case has to fold with a device that has three hinges bending in two
different directions and a folded shape that is a wedge rather than a slab.

The first vendor sample bound at hinge 3 before flat, which meant the device
could not reach the desk state at all with the case fitted. Sam has that open.
The redraw is three separate flexures rather than one spine mirrored, and the
case section tapers to match the real folded envelope.

Second sample is due later in September. All four finishes come off the same
tool geometry, so the fit fix is one fix rather than four, which is the piece of
good news in an otherwise annoying month.

## Questions people keep asking

**Can I buy a case in a colour that does not match my phone?**
Yes. Nothing stops it. We just do not merchandise it that way.

**Does the case work with the stand?**
Not with sample one, because sample one will not let the device reach the four
panel state. That is the same bug, not a second one.

**Why is the stand not adjustable?**
One angle means one detection profile, one thermal case and one moulding. An
adjustable stand is a different product and it is not this one.
