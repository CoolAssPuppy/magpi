## Why a page about this

We have a small number of pre production devices and each one costs more than
a car. Two of them are irreplaceable, because they are the units carrying a
cycle count we cannot rebuild without three weeks on a rig.

## Unit classes

| Class | Units | Who can touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cycle test | B7 retired, B11, B12, B13, B14 | Sam only. Nobody else moves these. |
| Lab | B9, B10 | Anyone in the lab |
| Carry | Seven engineering samples, one per person | The person it is signed out to |
| Golden | Two plus a spare, for the test house | Ben and Dana |

> A cycle test unit that gets picked up, opened and put down has lost its count.
> If you are not sure whether the unit in front of you is on a test, it is.

## Rules for carry units

- The unit is signed out to you. It stays with you.
- It does not leave the building unless the sign out says it can.
- No photographs of the device, in any state, anywhere. Not on a desk, not in a
  hand, not in the background of something else.
- It does not get shown to anybody outside the company. Not a partner, not a
  friend, not a family member who will absolutely not tell anyone.
- If it breaks, tell Sam. Breaking one is expected. Hiding a broken one wastes a
  week of somebody's debugging.
- If you lose it, tell Jane the same hour.

## Rules for the lab

1. The cycle benches run unattended overnight. Do not stop one to use the bench
   space.
2. Anything on a bench with a printed traveller next to it is mid test. Leave it.
3. Log any bench you use and when.
4. Raking light lamps live on the inspection bench and stay there.
5. Panels and coupons go back in their carton with the lot number. A polymer
   panel out of its lot is scrap.
6. Do not clean an outer panel with anything except the supplied cloth. The hard
   coat is 4H now and half the cleaning products in the kitchen will mark it.

**Can I take a unit to a customer meeting?**
Ask Jane. The answer is usually no and it is never yes at short notice.

**What about a screenshot rather than a photograph?**
Also no. A screenshot of the Ori DESK layout says as much about the device as a
photograph of it does.

**I broke the outer panel on my carry unit.**
Tell Sam, hand it in, and expect questions about how, because a real world break
is more interesting to us than a rig result.
