# Offsite notes, day one

Ben Achilov
2026-08-19

Jane asked someone who was not running the sessions to take the notes, so these
are mine. Two days, all seven of us, a rented room above a bike shop with a
whiteboard that does not erase properly. Day two notes are Dana's.

## Session one. What the Fold S1 is for

We spent ninety minutes on a question we thought was settled, which was whether
the third fold is a product or a demo.

The case for it being a product: a phone that opens to a tablet is a category
that exists and that people already understand. A tablet that opens again into
something the size of a desk mat is the only reason to pick us over a
two-panel device. Maya argued that the whole story is the third fold and that
without it we are a worse version of a phone that already ships.

The case against: every hard problem we have comes from the third hinge. The
thermal problem is a four-panel problem. The crease conversation is a
three-hinge conversation. The Bellows assembly cost is dominated by the third
hinge, and Dana pointed out that it is also the part with the longest lead
time and the fewest qualified suppliers.

Nobody proposed cutting it. Sam said the quiet thing, which is that if we cut
it we would ship six months earlier and nobody would remember us.

Outcome: no decision, because there was nothing to decide. Four panels.

## Session two. The outer layer

This is the session that mattered and we did not know it at the time.

Sam walked the group through the state of the hinge cycle test. Four units on
the rig, all four past 60,000 cycles, all four clean. UTG-3 had been chosen on
12 August. He was careful to say that the cycle data was incomplete and that he
was not comfortable with how confident the rest of us sounded.

I wrote down what he said because it turned out to be the whole month: "Glass
either survives the cycle count or it does not, and we will not know which one
until a unit tells us."

Maya asked what happens to the launch assets if the material changes. Sam said
they would need to be re-shot. Maya said that was a four week problem. Jane
said a four week problem is not a reason to pick a material.

Priya asked whether reviewers can tell polymer from glass by touch. Sam's
answer was that reviewers can tell everything by touch and that the question is
whether they care. Nobody had an answer to that.

## Session three. Ori

I ran this one so the notes are thin.

Panel handoff was demoed at two panels and stubbed at four. The room agreed the
target is that opening the device never shows a wrong layout, not even for one
frame. That is a hard target and I said so.

Open item: what Ori does when a panel is physically damaged and the touch
layer stops reporting. Currently it does something embarrassing. Logged.

## Session four. What we are afraid of

Jane ran a round where everyone said the thing they were most worried about.
Writing them down without names because that was the deal, except she told me
afterwards to keep hers attached.

- The hinge fails in the field after the review cycle is over.
- The factory says yes to a date they cannot hold.
- Certification finds a thermal problem in tablet mode.
- We build a beautiful thing that costs more than anyone will pay for it.
- One person leaves and takes a subsystem with them.
- The crease.

Jane's, on the record: that we are three separate companies. Hardware knows
what breaks, marketing knows what we promised, finance knows what it costs, and
the only place all three meet is her.

## Dinner

Sam brought a B-series unit and let people open and close it while they ate,
which he later said was an uncontrolled cycle test with pasta on it. Dana got
about four hundred cycles out of the table before Sam took it back.
