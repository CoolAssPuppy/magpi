# Board update, September, the markets section

Jane Okonkwo
2026-09-07
Section three of four. John writes section four and it is the numbers section.

This is the part of the September board update that covers where we are
selling, how much we think we will sell and who has committed to take it. I
have written it out separately because two board members asked for the market
detail after August and August did not have any.

Nothing here is a sales figure. We have not sold a device and will not for some
weeks yet. Everything below is a forecast, a piece of research or a partner
commitment, and I have marked which is which because the three deserve very
different levels of confidence.

## Where we are selling

Eleven markets, three tiers.

Tier 1 is five markets on launch day in November: Korea, Japan, the United
States, Germany and the United Kingdom.

Tier 2 is four markets six weeks later: France, the Netherlands, Canada and
Australia. Six weeks is the shortest gap that lets the local language builds
ship without a second firmware branch. That is Ben's number and I have not
argued with it.

Tier 3 is Singapore and the United Arab Emirates in 2027, with no committed
date and, at the moment, nobody assigned to the regulatory work. I would rather
tell you that than let it appear as a line on a slide with a quarter next to it.

## What we think we will sell

from three inputs and the strongest of the three is the smallest.

Korea and Japan together are 72,000 units, or 40 percent of the quarter. Two
markets carrying two fifths of the plan is a concentration and the board should
read it as one. The reason is that both markets have lived with folding devices
long enough that we are not explaining the category to them, and the panel bore
that out with an intent to consider ten points above the western markets.

## Who has committed

Two carriers, A and B. Both signed, neither exclusive, 71,000 units committed
between them against the 180,000.

Carrier A has committed 46,000 units across Korea, the United States, Germany
and Canada. Carrier B has committed 25,000 across Japan, the United Kingdom,
Australia and France. Neither schedule is countersigned yet and both are due to
be by 30 September.

Two things about that 71,000 are worth your attention.

It is just under 40 percent of the plan resting on two relationships, in a
first generation product, where neither partner has sold anything like this
before. If one of them halves their order in October we do not have eight weeks
to find someone else.

And it is the firmest number in the whole forecast. The parts of this plan I am
most confident about are the parts I control least.

## The four finishes

Four colourways ship: Ink, Chalk, Moss and Ember. Six went into review in
August and two were cut, both as consequences of the outer layer reversal
rather than as taste decisions. Tide would not hold its tint under the new
coating and Rust needed a second anodising pass with no tooling slot to do it
in.

The interesting result is Chalk, the warm off-white, at 26.7 percent of the
forecast. It is the second largest finish and it is second largest because it
shows the fold line least. People chose the finish where they could not find
the crease. That is the clearest evidence we have that the engineering work on
the crease has a commercial return, and it arrived from a consumer panel rather
than from a lab.

## The risks, in the order I would rank them

**One lab week in Korea.** Carrier A's national approval sits in a single lab
week in early October with no alternative slot before launch. Korea is 41,000
units and the single highest intent market in the study. This is the largest
concentrated risk in the launch and it is a scheduling risk rather than a
technical one, which does not make it smaller.

**A production cosmetic unit.** The review programme, the product photography
and the retail demonstration units all wait on the same hardware and it has
slipped twice. Three plans, one dependency.

**Seven people, nine markets.** We committed to five tier 1 markets when we had
four people who could run a market and we have the same four people. Nothing
has gone wrong yet. Nothing going wrong is not the same as having room.

**One vendor for the outer layer.** Not a market risk, but it sits behind every
number above, and Sam and Dana have raised it twice.

## What I would ask the board for

Nothing this month, and that is deliberate. The market plan is as built out as
seven people can make it and the open items are ours to close.

The question I would like put to me in October, when I will have a better
answer than I have now, is whether five tier 1 markets was the right count or
whether three done properly would have beaten five done at this staffing.
