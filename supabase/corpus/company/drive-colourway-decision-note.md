# Four colours, and why there are four

Jane Okonkwo
2026-09-09
Related: the colourway lineup decision, the finish durability results, GTM-31

## Why I am writing this down

Three people have asked me some version of "what happened to the blue one" in
the last fortnight, and two of them were not joking. One asked whether we cut it
because it did not test well, which is the opposite of what happened.

So here is the whole thing in one place, for everybody, once.

## The short version

The Fold S1 ships in four finishes. Ink, Chalk, Moss, Ember.

Six went into review on 24 August. Two of them, Tide and Rust, were cut in the
48 hours after we changed the outer layer from UTG-3 glass to Meniscus-C polymer
on 27 August. Neither cut was a taste decision. Both were consequences of the
material change, and if we had not made that change we would probably be
shipping six.

## The longer version

### We were only ever shipping four

Before either cut, marketing had already decided the lineup was four and not
six. That decision is on its own page from 26 August and the reasoning is worth
repeating, because it is the part everyone forgets.

A finish is not a colour. It is a coating recipe, a frame process, a case tool, a
set of packaging art, a line changeover, three cosmetic units for photography,
and eleven retail SKUs. Six finishes is sixty six SKUs across our markets. We are
seven people.

The panel work also says colour does not move whether somebody buys this
product. It moves which one they pick once they have decided. That makes a fifth
finish a stocking problem rather than a demand opportunity.

So the plan on 26 August was six into validation, four out, with hardware
deciding which four. What we did not know was which two we would lose.

### Tide

Tide was the pale blue and it was the best result in the panel. Broadest
positive across all five of our first markets, no strong negative anywhere, and
the finish most people in the review picked as their favourite.

The Suwon polymer arrives with a hard coat and an anti-fingerprint layer, and
that stack goes over the whole outer face and the rear cover rather than only
the display area. It sits on top of the colour on every finish. It is also
slightly warm, at b* +1.9 against +0.2 for the glass stack we were using before.

On a near black, a warm white, a deep green or a copper that is nothing. On a
pale blue it is the entire shade. Coated Tide panels read grey-green next to the
uncoated master, at delta E 4.6 against a gate of 1.5, which means a printer, a
case supplier and a swatch card could not have matched it to each other.

Sam tried three ways to save it and wrote all three down, including the one that
nearly worked. Suwon confirmed the cast is inherent to the chemistry rather than
a bad lot, in writing, quickly, which I want to note because a supplier that
tells you the awkward thing on the same day is worth keeping.

### Rust

Rust is the duller story and the one that annoys me more.

Rust passed everything. Colour holds, scratch holds, abrasion holds, light
fastness holds. It is the only one of the six that came out of validation clean
and got cut anyway.

Oxide red needs two anodising passes on the frame, a base pass and a second dyed
pass, because one pass comes out uneven at the hinge knuckles. That second pass
was going to be booked into the tooling window in the first week of September.
The polymer change meant a die reorder, the die reorder took the window, and
Dana looked twice for a second slot before the pilot line and there is not one.

The second pass is not difficult and it is not exotic. It is a booking, and we
did not have a place to put it.

## What this cost and what it bought

I said at the all hands on 28 August that a material change costs more than the
material, and this is the bill arriving. Two finishes, one of them the best
tested of the six, gone inside two days of a decision that had nothing to do
with colour.

What we bought is a phone whose outer layer does not fracture at 180,000 folds.
I would make the same trade in the same order every time, and I want everyone to
be able to see the trade rather than only the consequence.

## What we say outside this room

Marketing has the exact words and they are on the naming page. The version I
will use, and the version I would like everyone else to use, is this.

> Four finishes. We had six in review and cut two of them for manufacturing
> reasons when the outer layer material changed in August. We would rather ship
> four we are certain of than six where two are a coin toss on the line.

Two things we do not say. We do not name the cut colours, because an unshipped
colour with a name is a thing people ask for every week for a year. And we do
not promise a fifth finish in the spring, because nobody has decided that and
saying it out loud decides it.

## The part I want to keep

Every step of this is written down somewhere findable. The reversal is in a
decision record with the superseded choice still in it. The tint measurement is
in an issue with the plots and the three failed attempts. The tooling slot is in
an issue that says cancelled rather than blocked, with the reason in it. The
lineup logic is on a page that was written before we knew the outcome, and it
names the two finishes most at risk, correctly, a day early.

That last one is the one I am pleased about. Somebody wrote down what they
thought would happen, in a document, before it happened, and then did not go
back and quietly edit it.

If in six months somebody joins and asks why there are four colours, they should
be able to find every step of this without asking a person. That is the standard,
and on this one we hit it.
