## The week

| When | What | Who | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Hardware and ops sync | Sam, Dana, Jane | 30 minutes |
| Tuesday | Ori and firmware sync | Ben, Jane | 30 minutes |
| Wednesday | Marketing sync | Maya, Priya, Jane | 30 minutes |
| Thursday | Written update from each area, in the channel | Everyone | Written |
| Every second Friday | All hands | Everyone | 40 minutes |
| Monthly | Numbers review | John, Jane | 60 minutes |

That is four hours of scheduled meeting a week for most people and less for
some. Anything that needs more than this is a project, and a project gets a
Linear issue and a page rather than a recurring invite.

## The Thursday written update

Four lines per area, posted in the area channel. The format is deliberately
small so that people write it.

1. What moved this week.
2. What is stuck, and who could unstick it.
3. What is coming next week.
4. Anything that costs money or moves a date.

> Line four exists because a decision with a cost consequence that reaches
> finance after the fact costs more than the same decision reaching it before.
> The outer layer reversal in August is the good example. The tooling commit
> deadline got flagged on the Wednesday and the decision moved to the Thursday
> rather than the following week.

**Can I skip a sync if there is nothing to say?**
Yes. Say so in the channel and give the half hour back. A sync held because it
is in the calendar is where process starts eating a small company.

**Who chairs the all hands?**
Jane. Notes rotate and whoever takes them owns the page.
