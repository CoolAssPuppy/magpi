# Regional demand model, first quarter, build 4

Priya Raghunathan
2026-09-09
Build 4. Supersedes build 3 of 21 August, which had six colourways in it.
Related: GTM-24, GTM-29, market tiers, Korea and Japan weighting

## What this is

One number, 180,000 units, broken down far enough that every person who has to
order something can find their own line in it. First quarter means the thirteen
weeks from launch day, 4 November 2026, so tier 2 markets contribute about
seven weeks each and tier 1 contributes all thirteen.

Nothing in here is a sales report. The device is pre-production and nothing has
sold. Every number below is a forecast, a carrier commitment or a panel result,
and the three are marked so nobody quotes one as another.

Money is not in this document. If you need the money view, John has it and it
is built from these unit numbers.

## The one constraint

180,000 units across tiers 1 and 2. Korea and Japan together are 72,000 of it,
which is 40 percent. Every table in this document adds to those two numbers and
if one of them does not, the table is wrong and not the constraint.

Two things fall out of this table and both are somebody's problem.

Chalk at 26.7 percent is higher than any of us expected for an off-white, and
it is entirely because Chalk shows the crease least of the four. People who
looked at the panel photographs picked the finish where they could not find the
fold line. Dana needs to know that the second highest volume finish is the one
with the fussiest coating.

Ember at 13 percent looks small until you notice it is 23,435 units of a finish
that needs its own anodising slot. Ember has narrow appeal and very high intent
where it lands, which is the shape of a finish that sells out in three markets
and sits in the other six.

## Method

Three inputs, in the order I trust them.

**Carrier commitments.** Signed, in units, by market and by finish. 71,000
units. No modelling in this at all, it is a copy of what Alder and Birch put
their names to.

**Panel intent.** 1,800 respondents across the five tier 1 markets in the week
of 17 August, 360 per market. Intent converted to units using a category
conversion factor from the last two folding launches, discounted 30 percent
because a four panel device has no category to convert against.

**Comparable launches.** Two folding devices, both first generation, both in
our price band, both launched into tier 1 markets that overlap ours by four of
five. This is where the tier 2 numbers come from and it is why they are soft.

The three inputs disagree most in Germany. Panel intent puts Germany above the
United Kingdom by a wide margin, comparables put it barely above, and Alder's
commitment sits between them. I took the carrier number and let the other two
argue.

## What would break this model

The 40 percent Korea and Japan weighting depends on both carriers certifying on
time. Alder covers Korea and Birch covers Japan, and if either of them slips
past launch day we lose the launch window in a market that is a fifth of the
quarter on its own. There is no second carrier in either market to fall back on.

A finish cut after this build costs more than Tide and Rust did. Tide and Rust
came out before the packaging art went to print. A cut after the print date
means Chalk and Moss absorb the volume and neither has the coating capacity to
do it inside seven weeks.

The tier 2 date. Six weeks after launch is 16 December, which is the worst week
of the year to put a new device into European retail and the best week of the
year to put one into Australian retail. Nobody has asked whether we should
split tier 2 into two dates. I am asking now.

## The digression I owe you

Build 3 of this model had a Tide column in it and Tide was forecast at 8
percent. When I deleted the column I had to decide where 14,400 units went, and
the honest answer is that there is no method for that. A person who wanted pale
blue does not automatically want warm off-white. I pushed most of it into Chalk
and some into Ink, and the shape of the result looks fine, and I have no
evidence for it beyond it looking fine. If the Chalk number is wrong, this is
where it went wrong.
