# Why Korea and Japan are forty percent

Korea and Japan are 72,000 of the 180,000 unit first quarter forecast. Two
markets out of nine carrying 40 percent of the plan is the kind of number
somebody senior asks about, so here is the working.

## The three reasons

**Category familiarity.** Both markets have had folding devices on shelves
longer than anywhere else and both have a population that has already decided a
folding phone is a normal thing to own rather than a curiosity. We are not
explaining the category in Seoul or Tokyo. We are explaining what four panels
add to a category they already accept.

**The panel result.** Intent to consider was 31 percent in Korea and 29 percent
in Japan against 19 to 21 percent in the three western tier 1 markets. That gap
is wide enough to survive any discount we apply to it.

**Carrier weight.** Alder in Korea and Birch in Japan have committed 31,500
units between them, which is 44 percent of all carrier commitment across all
eight covered markets. Partners with local knowledge put their own inventory
behind these two markets before we asked them to.

## The panel, and what it actually was

1,800 respondents, five tier 1 markets, 360 per market, week of 17 August.
Online, screened for smartphone purchase in the last 36 months, no device
shown, three concept boards and a written description of the fold states.

360 per market gives roughly a 5 percent margin of error at market level, which
is fine for ranking markets against each other and not fine for splitting hairs
inside one.

## Segment share of intent, by market

The six segments are the same six the whole company uses. These are shares of
intent inside each market, so each column adds to 100.

| Segment | Korea | Japan | US | Germany | UK | All five |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commuter | 31% | 29% | 19% | 21% | 20% | 24% |
| Desk | 17% | 19% | 23% | 24% | 22% | 21% |
| Creator | 21% | 20% | 18% | 15% | 16% | 18% |
| Traveller | 13% | 12% | 17% | 17% | 16% | 15% |
| Field | 10% | 11% | 15% | 16% | 13% | 13% |
| First-in-line | 8% | 9% | 8% | 7% | 13% | 9% |

Commuter is where the two markets separate from the other three. Thirty percent
against twenty. Long commutes, high screen hours on public transport, and a
device that goes from pocket to tablet in one movement without needing a table.

The United Kingdom is the odd column. First-in-line at 13 percent against 7 to 9
everywhere else, on the smallest base in the study. I would not build a plan on
it and I have not.

## Colourway, and the Moss question

Moss is 30 percent of the Korean forecast and 28 percent of the Japanese
against 16 to 18 percent in the other seven markets.

| Market | Moss share | Moss units |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Korea | 30% | 12,300 |
| Japan | 28% | 8,680 |
| United States | 17% | 7,140 |
| Germany | 18% | 3,690 |
| United Kingdom | 18% | 2,520 |

Korea and Japan are 20,980 units of the 40,195 unit Moss total. Half of every
deep green device we build in the first quarter goes to two markets.

That was a panel result before it was a forecast. Moss came third on the
concept boards in the three western markets and first in both eastern ones, in
all three waves, with the finish shown against the same neutral background
every time. It is not a photography artefact.

Dana has the operational version of this: Moss has the longest coating lead of
the four shipping finishes, and the demand for it is concentrated in the two
markets whose carrier certifications are the least certain.

## What would make this number wrong

Korean certification slipping past launch day. Alder's lab week is 5 October
and there is no second slot. Losing Korea for the launch window takes 11,000
units of launch tranche and most of the reason the eastern weighting exists.

Panel intent not converting. Familiarity with the category cuts both ways. A
market that has seen four folding devices has also seen four folding devices
disappoint, and our discount for that is a judgement and not a measurement.
