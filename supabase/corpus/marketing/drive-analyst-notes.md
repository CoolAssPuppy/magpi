# Analyst calls, notes and what I take from them

Maya Restrepo
2026-09-04
Three calls, 1 to 3 September, all under NDA, no unit shown
Related: GTM-7, GTM-12

## Why we did these now

The category forecasts that matter get written in October and published in
November. An analyst who first hears about us on 4 November writes about us in
February. So three calls, under NDA, no device in the room, no date, no price,
and the narrowest possible description of what we are building.

I ran all three. Priya was on two of them and took better notes than these.

None of them are named in this document. If you need to know which is which,
ask me.

## Call one, the supply side analyst

Spends their days on panel and hinge supply and knows more about our suppliers
than we do.

Guessed the outer layer material inside four minutes, from the folded thickness
and the number of hinges, and was right about the vendor. Said the second
source question is the one they would ask any four panel device maker first,
and that a single line in a single building is what turns a good year into a
bad quarter. That was blunter than they meant it to be, because it is the risk
our own team has already flagged twice.

Was more interested in the hinge than in the display. Their view is that
everybody can buy a foldable panel and almost nobody can build a three hinge
assembly that survives, so the hinge is the defensible part of the product and
we underplay it.

Asked what our cycle specification is. I said two hundred thousand and they
said that is the right number and that most first generation devices claim more
than they have tested.

## Call two, the device analyst

Covers phones, writes the forecast most of the press quotes in the first week.

Opened by saying the four panel category does not exist and asking why we think
it should. That is the correct question and I was glad to get it on a call
rather than in a briefing.

What moved them: the desk state as a working surface, described as a thing you
put down and work at rather than a thing you hold up. What did not move them:
anything about the fold, which they have heard from four companies.

Their scepticism is about durability and about applications. On applications
they made a point I want to keep: a device with four panels is judged by
whether the software on it treats four panels as normal, and every previous
folding device has felt like a phone that had been stretched. I said our shell
was written for four panels from the first line of code and they said everybody
says that.

Asked where it prices. I did not have a number to give them and would not have
given it. They took a guess out loud that was $200 above where we are, and I
kept my face still, which is the hardest work I did all week.

## Call three, the channel analyst

Covers operators and retail.

Their whole message was about the calendar. Retail systems freeze in the middle
of November for the holiday period. A device that misses the freeze does not
launch in December, it launches in January into the worst month of the year for
a premium device. They said this without knowing our date, which is either
reassuring or unnerving.

Asked how many partners. I said more than one and fewer than five. They said a
first generation device with two partners is in a better position than one with
one, because the second partner is what makes the first one price properly.

Also said, unprompted, that a four figure device with a first generation hinge
will be sold on the story of what it does and not on the specification sheet,
and that the retail staff training material matters more than the launch video.
We have no retail staff training material.

## What I take from all three

**The hinge is the story and we are underselling it.** Two of the three came
back to it. We have a three hinge assembly that survives a specification most
first generation devices do not test to, and the launch narrative currently
gives it two sentences in section three. That is my error and I am fixing it.

**The second source question arrives early.** It came up unprompted on the
first call and it will come up in the briefings. We need an answer that is
true, short and does not sound like a shrug. Right now we do not have one.

**The software question decides the reviews.** Panel handoff working perfectly
is the difference between a device that reads as a real product and one that
reads as an engineering demonstration. Every hands-on session should show it
early and should show it working on a device the reviewer is holding.

**Nobody is worried about the crease.** All three brought it up and none of
them cared, because they assume it. The worry about the crease is ours.
