Six segments came out of the August panel. This page is what each name means,
so that when somebody writes Creator in a brief the next person reads the same
thing. The numbers are from LRG-26-0882 and the table volume that follows it.

## The six

| Segment | Age band | The one line | Share of intent | Base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commuter | 25 to 34 | Urban, high screen hours, wants the shut device to work | 24% | 432 |
| Desk | 35 to 54 | Wants the tablet state for documents | 21% | 378 |
| Creator | 18 to 29 | Wants the desk state for editing | 18% | 324 |
| Traveller | 30 to 44 | Dual SIM, flies often, reads on the device | 15% | 270 |
| Field | 30 to 49 | Buys on durability and asks for evidence | 13% | 234 |
| First-in-line | 22 to 40 | Buys because it is new | 9% | 162 |

Shares total 100 percent because the panel was screened to people already in
the category. A segment's share of the base and its share of intent are the
same number. Nobody should read these as shares of the phone market.

## What each name covers

**Commuter.** The largest segment and the one that lives in the phone state.
Sixty two percent of them expect to use the shut device most on an ordinary
day, the highest phone number in the study. They are not against the desk
state, they just do not picture themselves opening a device to full width on a
train. Anything we write for Commuter has to work on a device that never opens.

**Desk.** Older, works in documents, and chose the tablet state at 61 percent,
the single largest cell in the fold state table. This is the segment that
carries two devices today and resents the second one. Strongest in the United
States. It also has the highest Chalk preference of the six.

**Creator.** The desk state segment. Eighteen percent of the base and 46
percent of everyone who picked the desk state. Concentrated in Korea and Japan,
where it indexes 125 and 114, and thin in Germany and the United Kingdom, where
it indexes 74 in both. The only segment where Moss beats Ink.

**Traveller.** Dual SIM, frequent flights, and the segment whose open ends are
most about the device shut and in a bag. They ask about thickness more than any
other group. Even across markets, which makes them the easiest segment to write
one piece of copy for.

**Field.** Lowest intent of the six at 38.9 percent, and the segment that is
most specific about what would change its mind. They do not ask whether the
device is tough. They ask what happens after two years, what the fold line looks
like on a used unit, and what the warranty covers. Heaviest in Germany, where
it is the second largest segment in the market. Claims for this group go through
Sam before they go anywhere.

**First-in-line.** Smallest and hottest. Top two box intent of 72.2 percent
against 50.7 percent for the base. Twenty seven percent of them pick Ember, and
Ember buyers overall run at 74 percent intent. This is the segment that turns up
on day one whatever we do, which is a reason to spend less on them rather than
more.

## Naming rules

**The segment called Desk and the fold state called desk are two different
things.**
Ladderwell flagged this in the table volume and they are right. In any document
that leaves the building, the segment is capitalised and the state is not, and
if that feels too subtle then use the full phrase. Desk buyers mostly want the
tablet state, which is exactly the sentence that goes wrong when the two words
are next to each other.

**Segment names never appear in public.**
Same rule as Bellows, Meniscus and Ori. These are planning names. No press
release, no retail sign and no product page says the word Commuter.

**Do not invent a seventh.**
If a brief needs a group that is not one of these six, that is a signal the
brief is describing a use case rather than a buyer. Write the use case.

**Can I use a segment name in a supplier brief?**
Yes, agencies and photographers get them because they need them. Fieldhouse has
the full definitions. It stops at people who are working on the launch.

## What the panel does not tell us

- No price was shown, so none of these shares are willingness to pay. There is
  no number on this page that answers a pricing question and there was never
  going to be.
- Nobody handled a device. Every durability answer is a category expectation
  carried in from other folding phones.
- Four finishes were shown, not six. The two that were not rendered in time are
  absent from every table and always will be.
- The base is category qualified, so intent is measured among people already
  open to a strange shape. The absolute level is a ceiling.
