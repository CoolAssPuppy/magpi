## Shape of the briefing

Thirty minutes. Twenty of it is the device in the room and the reviewer holding
it. Ten is slides. A briefing where the reviewer does not open the device with
their own hands is a briefing they will write from the press release.

| Minute | What |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | Who we are, seven people, one product |
| 3 to 8 | The argument, in the four point order from positioning |
| 8 to 20 | Hands on. They open it. We say nothing for the first minute. |
| 20 to 26 | Software, the six panel states, the demo script |
| 26 to 30 | Questions, availability, embargo terms |

> The first minute of hands on is silent. Every person who has picked this
> device up has done the same thing, which is open it one panel at a time and
> stop at three. Let them do it.

## What every reviewer will ask

**Does it crease?**
Say what is true. The outer layer is a polymer chosen for how it behaves at our
bend radius, and the device is tested against a 200,000 cycle spec for a two
year life. Do not quote a test result that is still running. Sam clears any
number before it is said out loud.

**Why four panels and not two?**
Because two panels give you a slightly larger phone and four give you a surface.
This is the positioning argument and it is the answer we want them to write down.

**Is the outer screen glass?**
It is a polymer. Answer it plainly and move on. A reviewer who senses a dodge
will spend their whole review on the material.

**What does it cost?**
Price is not in this document and not in this deck yet. John owns the number and
it goes into the press materials two weeks out. Until then the answer in the
room is that pricing comes at launch.

**When can I buy one?**
The date is under embargo and the briefing itself covers it. Do not say the date
in any room where the embargo has not been signed.

**Which carriers?**
Two, both signed, and each announces on its own schedule. Use the codenames in
any written follow up.

it. Meniscus, Bellows and Ori do not appear in anything a reviewer sees.
