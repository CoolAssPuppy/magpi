# Retail readiness, where we actually are

Maya Restrepo
2026-09-04

Written because Jane asked me in the Wednesday sync what would stop us being in
shops, and I gave a worse answer than the question deserved. This is the better
answer. It covers packaging, accessories and the display units, which are the
three things that have to physically exist in a room somebody walks into.

## The short version

Nothing here is late yet. Two things are one slipped round away from being
late, and both of them are print. Everything with a factory attached is Dana's
date and Dana has not raised a flag, which I am choosing to read as good news
and also checking again next week.

## Packaging

The pack is moulded fibre with no plastic anywhere in it. Sleeve, tray,
literature wallet, paper band around the cable. Four sleeve variants for the
four shipping colourways and two accessory boxes on the same family.

Two things bind. First, the colourway names have to be signed before the art
releases, because the name is set in type on the sleeve front and the end flap.
Priya has that as GTM-27 and it should close this week. Second, the proof has
to be on the stock we actually print on. We already lost a round to that. The
sample we approved in August was on a substitute stock because the right one
was out, and on the real matte the fold line art in the sleeve print reads as a
hard black line across the front of the box. It looks like a scratch. On a
folding phone. I do not need to explain why that particular optical accident is
the one we cannot ship.

Priya's fix if the second proof fails is to drop the line art and print a plain
sleeve. I have signed off on that in advance so we do not spend a day debating
it in the week it matters.

The tray was harder than any of us expected. A folded Fold S1 is a thick wedge
rather than a flat slab, thicker at the outer hinge end, and moulded fibre
holds a looser tolerance than the plastic tray it replaced in the first
concept. The first tray was an interference fit on about half the samples. Sam
and Dana have signed the second one. Engineering is drop testing with the real
tray rather than a foam block, which is the right way round and which we did
not ask for.

## Accessories

Folio case in Ink, Chalk, Moss and Ember. Desk stand in Chalk only.

The case is the harder product. It has to wrap a four panel device with three
hinges, fold with it through phone, tablet and desk, and not bind on any of the
three. Sam's team has HW work open on hinge clearance and the last I heard the
outer hinge was the tight one. If the case fouls the fold, the case does not
ship, and a folio case that ships in three finishes instead of four is a
merchandising problem I would rather not hand the carriers.

The desk stand is Chalk only because a stand is furniture and furniture is not
a colour match exercise. It also has to be seen by the shell so the phone
switches to desk state when you set it down, which is the ORI issue Ben has.
When it works it is the best thirty seconds of the demo. When it does not, it
is a piece of moulded plastic that a customer paid for.

There is one thing I want written down. The desk stand only works with the
device in the four panel state, and the four panel state is the state that runs
hottest. Ben knows. I am noting it because if there is a thermal limitation on
the accessory it is the kind of thing that reaches me through a review rather
than through a meeting.

## Retail display units

Two hundred and forty units across tier 1, the exact split still moving with
the carriers. Each one holds a live device on a tether and invites a stranger
to fold it.

That last clause is the whole engineering problem. A display unit in a phone
shop gets handled a few hundred times a day by people who are not gentle and
who have no reason to be. Our device has three hinges rated for 200,000 cycles
over a two year life. A display unit does not get two years of normal use. It
gets a compressed and hostile version of it, from people testing exactly the
thing they have been told is fragile.

I have asked for the display fixture to hold the device in the tablet state
rather than folded shut, so the first thing a person does is open it further
rather than pry it apart. Priya has the fixture drawings out with the display
vendor and there is a Notion page on it.

## Dates

Retail is 4 and 7 November. The embargo lifts at 10:00 on 4 November. Display
units have to be in stores and built before the first of those dates, which
means they ship from the vendor in October, which means the fixture design
freezes in September. That is the chain and the front of it is now.

One, GTM-27 closes this week with signatures on it. Priya has it and it is not
blocked on anything but us.

Two, somebody who is not me asks Dana directly for the factory packaging date
in writing rather than everyone assuming it is comfortable. I have said twice
in a channel that I do not have it in front of me, which is a bad habit.

Three, engineering tells us this month whether the folio case clears all three
hinges in all three states, because a case that ships in three finishes and a
case that ships in four are different conversations with the carriers and I
want to have the right one early.

Jane, the honest answer to your question is the second proof. If it comes back
on the correct stock and still looks like a scratch, we go plain, and plain has
never once been the thing that hurt us.
