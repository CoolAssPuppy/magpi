## What we are making

One box design, four colourway variants, plus two accessory boxes. Moulded
fibre throughout. There is no plastic in the pack, not a tray, not a window,
not a bag around the cable. That was a decision made early and it has held
through three rounds of cost pressure, so it is not up for discussion again.

| Part | Material | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer sleeve | 350 gsm uncoated board, matte | Carries the colourway name and the fold line art |
| Tray | Moulded fibre, single piece | Holds the device folded, plus a cable well |
| Literature wallet | Folded card, no envelope | Safety sheet, warranty card, setup QR |
| Cable and brick | Paper band, no bag | Band is printed, not stickered |
| Folio case box | Moulded fibre, half depth | Same sleeve family, colourway name on the end |
| Desk stand box | Moulded fibre, deeper well | Chalk only, so one variant |

## The tray is the hard part

A folded Fold S1 is thicker than a normal phone by a wide margin. Four panels
and three hinges stack, and the folded block is not a flat slab. It is slightly
wedge shaped, thicker at the outer hinge end. Sam gave us the folded envelope
drawing on 26 August and the first tray we had moulded to it was too tight at
the hinge end and too loose at the far end.

Moulded fibre does not hold tolerance the way a vacuum formed plastic tray
does. The moulder quotes plus or minus 0.5 mm on a feature this size, and our
first tray had a 0.4 mm nominal clearance, which means half the trays were
interference fits. A device that has to be pushed into its own box is a bad
first ten seconds.

Second tray has 1.2 mm clearance at the hinge end and a compliant fibre rib
along the far edge that takes up the slack. Sam and Dana have both seen it.
Engineering is running the drop test with the real tray rather than a foam
stand-in, which is HW work and not ours, but the result changes our tray if it
comes back badly.

## Colourway names have to be locked first

This is the gate everything else waits behind.

The sleeve carries the colourway name in type. Ink, Chalk, Moss and Ember. If
any of those four words changes after the art goes to the printer, we are
reprinting sleeves and not just re-running a file. Four sleeve variants at
launch volume is not a small reprint.

> **Gate.** Colourway names locked and signed before packaging art releases to
> print. No exceptions, no "we can change it in the second run".

The four are the four that survived the review on 24 August. Tide and Rust were
cut, both for reasons that came out of the outer layer reversal rather than out
of taste, and neither is coming back for this launch. That means the risk here
is not that a colour gets added. It is that someone decides Chalk should be
called something warmer, six days before the file goes out.
