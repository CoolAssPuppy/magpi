## What this decides

Four finishes ship. This page says what they are called in public, what one line
we write under each of them, and what we say when someone asks why there are
four and not six.

> **Decision.** The internal shade words become the public names. Ink, Chalk,
> Moss, Ember. No invented words, no market-specific names, no numbers.

## The four

| Name | Shade | One line | Where it leads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | near black | "The one in every photograph." | Hero. Every render, every press image, the demo unit on every table |
| Chalk | warm off-white | "Warm white, and the fold line is hardest to find on this one." | The finish we hand to anyone who is going to photograph the fold line |
| Moss | deep green | "Deep green, closer to a coat than to a phone." | Korea and Japan, which are about 40 percent of the first quarter forecast |
| Ember | burnt copper | "Copper, and it is not for everyone." | Creator and First-in-line, where it out-performs everything else we tested |

Accessories follow the four. A folio case in each shipping finish, and the desk
stand in Chalk only, because a stand that has to match four phones is four
tools for an accessory nobody chooses on colour.

## Why these words and not new ones

We spent an afternoon on invented names and threw all of them away. Three tests
and the invented ones failed at least two each.

1. **Say it down a phone line.** A person orders the phone from a carrier
   assistant reading off a screen. Ink survives that. Anything with a silent
   letter does not.
2. **Translate or leave alone.** Ink, Chalk, Moss and Ember are concrete nouns.
   A concrete noun either has a clean equivalent in a market or stays in
   English without looking like a mistake. An invented word looks like a
   mistake in every market at once.
3. **Say what the thing looks like.** A person who has never seen the phone
   should be able to picture the colour from the word. Moss is a green. Ember
   is a warm orange-red. Nobody has to learn anything.

The one we argued longest about was Ember. It is the only name of the four that
describes a state rather than a material, and it is also the finish that people
who like it really like. Keeping it.

## Why four and not six

This is the question we will get, from reviewers and from retail, and both
answers have to be the same answer.

Six finishes went into review on 24 August. Two of them did not survive the
outer layer material change on 27 August, when Meniscus went from UTG-3 glass
to the Suwon polymer.

- **Tide**, a pale blue, was cut because the polymer hard coat is not optically
  neutral. Under the coating the blue read yellow-green. Hardware measured it
  and the shift was outside anything a print or a swatch could be matched to.
- **Rust**, an oxide red, was cut because it needed a second anodising pass on
  the frame, and the tooling slot that pass would have used went to the polymer
  die reorder.

Neither was a taste decision and neither of them was cut because we did not like
it. Both cuts came from the same material change that made the phone better.

### The public answer

> "Four finishes. We had six in review and cut two of them for manufacturing
> reasons when the outer layer material changed in August. We would rather ship
> four we are certain of than six where two are a coin toss on the line."

### What we do not say

- We do not name Tide or Rust. An unshipped colour with a name is a colour
  people ask for every week for a year.
- We do not describe the yellow shift. It is a true, specific, technical fact
  that reads as an admission the coating is tinted, and the coating is not
  tinted, the pale blue was just the one shade that showed it.
- We do not promise a fifth finish. Spring is a real option and it is not a
  thing we say out loud in November.

**Can a carrier have a fifth finish of its own?**
No. Both carriers asked. A carrier exclusive finish is a fifth tooling set, a
fifth case, a fifth set of packaging art and a support line explaining to
everyone else why they cannot have it.

**Can retail choose which of the four they stock?**
Yes, and most of them will. Ink and Chalk are the two we expect everywhere.
Moss and Ember are the two that will be uneven by market, and that is fine.

## Names have to be locked before the art goes out

Packaging is a moulded fibre tray with no plastic in it, and the name is printed
on the sleeve rather than on a sticker. That means the four words on this page
are set before the packaging art goes to print in October, and a name change
after that is a reprint of every sleeve for every finish in eleven markets.

Which is the whole reason this page exists on 9 September rather than in October.
