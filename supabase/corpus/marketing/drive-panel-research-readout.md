# Panel readout, what 1,800 people told us

Maya Restrepo
2026-08-26

Ladderwell delivered the topline on Monday. Priya and I have been through it
twice and this is what I would say if you gave me twenty minutes and a room.
The tables volume is still two weeks out, so anything here that depends on a
crosstab is provisional and marked.

I want to start with the thing that surprised me, because the rest of the
report mostly confirmed what we already believed and confirmation is boring.

## The desk state is the concept and not the habit

We asked people, after the animation, which of the three shapes they thought
they would use most on an ordinary weekday. Forty one percent said tablet.
Thirty seven percent said phone. Twenty two percent said desk.

I had expected the desk state to win. It is the thing nobody else has, it is
the thing every render leads with, and it is the reason the product exists.
Twenty two percent.

The obvious reading is that we have overweighted it. I think that reading is
wrong and here is why. When we removed the renders and asked people to describe
the device in their own words, sixty one percent mentioned a size or a surface.
Twelve percent mentioned folding without mentioning size. The desk state is
what makes the device memorable even for people who do not expect to use it
daily. It is what gets the product
understood. The tablet state is what gets used.

That is not a contradiction. It is how a flagship feature works in a category
where most days are ordinary. But it changes what we ask each asset to do. The
film sells the desk state. The retail unit and the product page have to sell the
tablet state, because that is the shape a person is imagining themselves in when
they decide.

## Six segments, and one of them is a trap

The clustering gave six groups. Commuter is the biggest at twenty four percent,
then Desk at twenty one, Creator at eighteen, Traveller at fifteen, Field at
thirteen, First-in-line at nine. Priya has the definitions written up.

The trap is the segment called Desk, which mostly does not want the desk state.
Desk buyers are thirty five to fifty four, work in documents, and picked the
tablet state at sixty one percent, the highest single number anywhere in the
report. Ladderwell put a warning in about it and they were right to. Two of our
own words, one meaning a person and one meaning a shape, sitting next to each
other in a brief, is a sentence going wrong in a meeting we are not in.

The one that actually matters for creative is Creator. Eighteen percent of the
sample, and forty six percent of everyone who chose the desk state. If we want
the desk state on film, that film is talking to Creator and we should stop
pretending otherwise and cast it accordingly.

## Field is the segment we are worst at and the one we can fix

Thirteen percent of the sample, and the lowest intent of the six at thirty nine
percent against fifty one overall. Heaviest in Germany.

The instinct is to write Field off as the sceptics. Read the open ends and that
is not what is happening. They are not sceptical, they are specific. They want
to know what the device looks like after two years, not whether it is durable.
One of them asked to see a used unit rather than a new one. Another asked
whether there is a bump in the middle you can feel with your thumb, yes or no.

Those are answerable questions. Not with adjectives, which is why every
durability line we have tried so far has sounded defensive. They are answerable
with the cycle test, with a photograph of a unit that has been folded a hundred
thousand times, and with a warranty someone has actually read.

I am going to say the uncomfortable part. We cannot answer any of them today,
because the cycle run is still going and the number at the end of it is not
ours to promise. Sam has been clear about that and he is right. So the Field
work is blocked on hardware, and the honest plan is to hold the segment until
the test finishes rather than write something that will not survive contact
with a reviewer.

## Colours

Ink took thirty nine percent of first choice, Moss twenty seven, Chalk twenty
three, Ember eleven.

Moss is a Korea and Japan result. It is first choice in both, ahead of Ink, and
third everywhere else. Those two markets carry the largest single share of the
first quarter plan, which means the colour that wins where the volume is is not
the colour in our hero render. I do not think that means we change the hero. I
think it means the Korea and Japan assets are Moss and we stop treating regional
variation as a compromise.

Ember is the interesting one. Eleven percent of first choice, the smallest of
the four, and the people who pick it are at seventy four percent intent against
fifty one overall. Ladderwell's warning is worth quoting because I will
otherwise get this wrong in six weeks: choosing Ember does not cause intent,
Ember is what people who already want the device pick. Making more Ember does
not make more buyers. What it does mean is that a finish nobody can find in a
store is a finish those people do not buy, and the retail allocation
conversation should hear that.

Ink is nobody's favourite and everybody's second favourite. It never drops
below a third of any market and it never wins one convincingly. That is exactly
what a lead finish should look like and I would be worried if it were not true.

## What I am not going to do with this

No price question was fielded. That was our instruction and it was the right
one, and it means there is no number in this report that answers a pricing
question. When somebody quotes the fifty one percent intent figure back at me
in a planning meeting I am going to say, out loud, that concept intent without
price is a ceiling and not a forecast. Ladderwell said so first and put it in
writing.

Nobody handled a device. Every crease answer in here is a memory of a different
phone.

And four finishes were shown, not six. Two were not rendered in time for
fieldwork. There is nothing in this study about them and I do not want to see a
slide implying otherwise.

## What changes on Monday

1. Creative brief for the film is written to Creator, on the desk state.
2. Product page and retail are written to Desk and Commuter, on the tablet and
   phone states.
3. Korea and Japan assets lead Moss. Everywhere else leads Ink.
4. Field work is parked until the cycle test finishes, and Priya and I write the
   list of questions we would need answered rather than the copy we cannot
   write yet.
5. Nobody spends anything on First-in-line. They are coming anyway.

Priya is turning items one to three into briefs this week. Item four is a page
of questions for Sam and I will hand it over rather than send it, because it
reads like an interrogation on paper.
