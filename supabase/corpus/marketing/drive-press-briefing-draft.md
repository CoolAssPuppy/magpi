# Press briefing, draft 3

Priya Raghunathan
2026-09-01
For the twelve hands-on sessions, the two weeks before 4 November
Related: GTM-12, GTM-15

## Before anything else

Every briefing is under embargo to 10:00 on 4 November. That is on the calendar
invitation, it is said out loud at the start of the session, and it is on the
first page of the materials. Everyone has the same time. One outlet will ask
for an early lift three times and the answer is the same on all three
occasions.

Twelve sessions, forty-five minutes each, in this office, with a real unit that
the person holds. Roughly forty more get a thirty minute video briefing with no
unit, and everybody else gets materials.

Two of us in the room for each session. One to run it and one to answer the
technical questions, because every reviewer will ask about the fold line inside
four minutes and the honest answer needs somebody who ran the test.

## The forty-five minutes

**Minutes 0 to 3. Embargo and housekeeping.** Say the date and time. Say that
photography in the room is allowed and that we will send better images than
they can take here. Say that the unit is pre-production.

**Minutes 3 to 8. The one sentence and the demonstration.** A phone that opens
into a tablet, and then opens again. Do not explain it first. Open it, in one
movement, and let the room be quiet for a second.

**Minutes 8 to 20. They hold it.** This is the session. Hand it over early,
stop talking, and let them open and close it as many times as they want. Every
question they ask while holding it is worth more than anything on a slide.

**Minutes 20 to 32. The three states, in use.** Phone, tablet, desk. Show a
real task in the desk state with three applications side by side, and show the
handoff: open the device and the thing you were looking at is where it should
be, without a flash of the wrong layout.

**Minutes 32 to 42. Questions.**

**Minutes 42 to 45. What happens next.** Materials arrive within the hour. A
review unit ships to the ones getting one. Contact details for follow-up
questions, and an offer to get an engineer on a call, which we should mean.

## The questions we will get, and the answers

**Is that a crease?**

Yes. Every folding device has a fold line and ours is there. Here is a
photograph of it under a hard light at the angle where it shows most, with the
cycle count printed next to it. The outer layer is a polymer and it creases
less than the glass we tested. Offer the photograph before they ask for it.

**Why not glass on the outside at this price?**

The glass we could get cracked at 180,000 folds on the hinge cycle rig, against
a 200,000 fold specification, which is two years of ordinary use. We changed
the material. Say it plainly, with the number, and stop talking. Do not turn
this into a story about the glass failing.

**How many folds will it survive?**

Two hundred thousand, which is our acceptance specification, roughly two years
of ordinary use at 270 opens a day. Do not say the device will last five years.
Do not invent a number for the polymer that engineering has not given us.

**Does it get hot?**

In the full desk state with four panels lit it runs warm and stays inside the
limit we set for a surface a person holds. If they push, get the engineer to
answer. Do not describe the firmware behaviour that makes this true, because
the shortest accurate description of it sounds worse than it is.

**What does it cost?**

Pricing comes with the announcement. This is the answer in every session, in
every format, to every version of the question, including the one that starts
with "off the record".

**Who is selling it?**

Two partners, both announcing on the day. One is first to offer it in retail by
three days, the other is a launch partner from day one. Both are named in the
materials and neither is exclusive.

**Is this a real product or a concept?**

It goes on sale on 4 November. Fair question, badly meant, and the answer is a
date.

**What happens if I drop it?**

It has been drop tested and there are conditions in which an inner panel
cracks. Do not volunteer this. If asked directly, say that drop testing is
ongoing and offer the engineer, because a half answer here is worse than no
answer.

## Things not to say

- Any number that is not in this document or in the materials.
- The internal names for the outer layer, the hinge assembly or the shell.
- Any comparison to a named competitor's hinge.
- Anything about the second generation.
- Any date other than 4 November.

## What I need before the first session

The final photograph set including the fold line image. The engineer named for
each of the twelve slots. A unit that holds a half fold position without
drifting, because two people will stand it on a table and photograph it. And
a decision from Jane on the number I am not allowed to say, in time for the
materials to be built around it.
