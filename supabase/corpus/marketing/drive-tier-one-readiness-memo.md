# Tier one readiness, five markets, eight weeks out

Maya Restrepo
2026-09-08
For Jane. Copied to Priya and Ben.
Related: GTM-24, GTM-29, market tiers

## The ask

You asked me last Thursday which of the five tier 1 markets I would bet on
being ready on launch day and which I would not. Here is the honest version,
market by market, with the thing that would stop each one.

Eight weeks and change to 4 November. Embargo lifts at 09:00 Eastern that
morning and every market below has to have a device in a door within hours of
that hour.

## Korea, 41,000 units

The largest single market by intent and the one I am least sure of.

Alder's national type approval sits in a lab week beginning 5 October. There is
no second slot before November. Ben has been clear that we can be ready for that
week and equally clear that being ready for it is not the same as passing it.

If Korea does not clear, we lose 11,000 units of Alder launch tranche and 12,300
units of Moss demand in the market that wants Moss most. The rest of the plan
does not have a place to put those units in eight weeks.

Everything else in Korea is fine. Retail is signed, the Korean build has been
in the shell since August, and the local agency has had the campaign since the
first week of September.

Ready, conditional on one lab week I do not control.

## Japan, 31,000 units

Birch have booked their own lab and have not told us which week, which is
either normal for them or a problem, and I cannot tell which from here.

The Japanese assets are the furthest ahead of any market. The desk state
demonstration was rebuilt for a lower table height after the agency saw the
first cut and said the working posture was wrong, and it is now the best piece
of film we have in any market.

Packaging is the open item. Japanese retail expects a finish name in Japanese
on the box, and the colourway names lock before the packaging art goes to
print. Nobody has translated Moss and Ember yet and the print date is closer
than the translation.

Ready, with a packaging item that has a hard date on it.

## United States, 42,000 units

The largest volume market and the least interesting to write about, which is
the correct state for a market eight weeks out.

FCC is engineering's line and Ben has the accredited lab in the week of 21
September. Alder's 16,500 unit commitment is the largest single-market number
anyone has put in writing.

The press are here, the reviewers are here, and the reviewer briefings begin
five weeks out, which is 30 September. Review units are the constraint and they
need production cosmetics, which means a unit built with the polymer outer
panel and the final coating. We do not have one yet.

Ready, except that the review programme depends on hardware that does not exist
on 8 September.

## Germany, 20,500 units

The market where my three forecast inputs disagree most and the one I would
most like a second opinion on.

Panel intent puts Germany well above the United Kingdom. Comparable launches
put it barely above. Alder's 8,000 unit commitment sits between the two and I
took the carrier number because a partner with money on the table is a better
estimator than I am.

The operational item is returns. German retail returns culture is the strictest
we will meet and a first generation device with a visible fold line will be
returned by people who read a review before they read the product page. We have
no returns process beyond a distributor's standard terms, and seven people
cannot staff one.

Ready to sell. Not ready to be returned to.

## United Kingdom, 14,000 units

The smallest tier 1 market and the one that pays for itself in coverage rather
than units.

Fourteen thousand units against a review density that is close to the United
States. If the British reviews go well they get quoted in five other markets
and if they go badly the same thing happens.

First-in-line came out at 13 percent here against 7 to 9 percent everywhere
else, on 360 respondents, which is thin. I have not built anything on it.

Birch has 5,500 units and UKCA is paperwork rather than a lab.

Ready.

## The three things that are actually blocking

Stripped of market detail, there are three.

1. **Korean approval, week of 5 October.** One lab week, no alternative, 11,000
   units of launch tranche behind it.
2. **A production cosmetic unit.** The review programme, the photography and
   the retail demonstration units all wait on the same hardware. Three
   different plans, one dependency, and it has slipped twice.
3. **Colourway names in five languages before the packaging print date.**
   Boring, cheap, entirely within our control, and nobody owns it.

The third one is the one that will embarrass us, because it is the one that has
no technical excuse.

## What I would do

Put the colourway translation on Priya this week and take something else off
her. Ask Dana for a date on the first production cosmetic unit and treat it as
a launch blocker rather than a photography inconvenience. Get Birch to name
their lab week, in writing, before the schedules are countersigned on 30
September.

Korea I would leave alone. Ben knows what the week of 5 October means and
adding pressure to it does not make a radio pass.

## One thing I want on the record

We committed to five tier 1 markets in July when we had four people who could
run a market and we now have the same four people. Nothing has gone wrong. I
want it written down that nothing going wrong is not the same as having slack,
and that the first thing to break will be whichever market's local agency needs
an answer on a day when all four of us are in a certification meeting.
