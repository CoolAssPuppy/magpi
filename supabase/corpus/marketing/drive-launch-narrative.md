# Fold S1 launch narrative

## What this document is for

Every other piece of launch material is built from this one. The press
briefing, the website, the carrier one pagers, the sizzle script and the twelve
hands-on sessions all say the same thing in different lengths, and this is
where the thing they say is written down.

If a sentence in this narrative is wrong, everything downstream of it is wrong
in the same way, which is why it took three weeks to write four pages.

## 1. The date

Launch is 4 November 2026. Embargo lifts at 10:00 that morning and every
outlet, analyst and partner has the same embargo time. Nobody gets an early
lift.

The date was locked on 20 August, working backwards from two constraints. The
retail systems we ship through freeze for the holiday period on 14 November, so
anything after that slips to January. The two big competing announcements are
in the last week of October, and being second in the same week means being the
second paragraph in somebody else's story.

4 November is a Wednesday, which is the best day of the week for press, nine
days clear of the freeze, and two weeks after the competition, which is far
enough that we are our own story.

Everything in the launch plan now counts backwards from that date. Final files
to partners are due 20 October and that is the date I will keep repeating until
people are sick of hearing it.

## 2. The one sentence

A phone that opens into a tablet, and then opens again.

That is the whole product. It is the sentence that goes at the top of the
release, it is the first thing said in every briefing, and it is what we want
somebody to repeat to a colleague the next day without having read anything
else.

## 3. Why anybody should care

Two-panel folding phones exist and they are good. The reason to buy ours is the
third fold, which turns the device into a working surface the size of a desk
mat.

The argument is about what a person does with it. A tablet is a thing you hold.
The full open state is a thing you put down and work at, with three or four
applications side by side, on a device that started the morning in a pocket.
Nobody has shipped that, and we should be careful to claim it as a first
without saying the word first more than once.

We call the three states phone, tablet and desk. Those are the ordinary words
and we tested three sets of invented names against them. Everybody understood
the ordinary words immediately, nobody guessed what the invented ones meant,
and two people thought one of them was a separate product. Ordinary words it is.

## 4. The outer layer, and the fold line

Rewritten 2 September. The August version of this section said glass and it
should be read as history.

The outer layer of the Fold S1 is a polymer. The engineering team tested
ultra-thin glass on the hinge cycle rig and it cracked at 180,000 folds,
against a specification of 200,000, which is two years of ordinary use. They
changed the material.

The public answer to why a phone at this price does not use glass on the
outside is that sentence, said plainly, with the number in it. Two years of
folding is a thing a person can picture. A team that tested to failure and
changed its mind reads better than a team that never tested, and reviewers can
tell the difference.

Two cautions for anyone writing from this. The claim is about what we chose,
and "the glass cracked" is not the headline we want, so lead with the polymer
and the cycle number. Second, the internal names for these parts stay internal.
No customer will ever hear the words we use in the building for the outer
layer, the hinge assembly or the shell software.

On the fold line itself: it exists, every folding device has one, and ours is
shallower than the glass version would have been. We will hand reviewers a
photograph of it under a hard light with the cycle count printed next to it. A
reviewer who finds it themselves has made a discovery. A reviewer who is handed
it with a number has been given engineering.

## 5. Who we are talking to

In order of how much of my time they get.

The twelve people who will hold a unit in this office for forty-five minutes.
Everything else is downstream of what they write.

The two partners who are selling it, whose systems, retail dates and legal
review processes set half the calendar. One of them is first to offer the
device by three days and the other is a launch partner from day one, and the
language that makes both of those true at once is drafted and with their legal
teams.

The person who has read three folding phone reviews, believes the category is
a gimmick, and is right about the devices they have seen.

## 6. What we do not say

We do not say the fold line is invisible. It is not, and the first photograph
after the embargo lifts settles that argument for us.

We do not say a price in any document until Jane releases the number. Not in a
deck, not in a carrier one pager, not in an answer to a direct question in a
briefing. The answer to the price question in a briefing is that pricing comes
with the announcement.

We do not use the word magic, we do not compare our hinge to a competitor
by name, and we do not promise a software feature that is not in the build the
reviewer is holding.
