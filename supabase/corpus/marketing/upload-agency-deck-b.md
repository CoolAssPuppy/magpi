SLIDE 3  The problem with folding phones

Every folding phone ad in the last four years is the same ad. A device opens in
slow motion. Music swells. Nothing happens.

The category has taught people that a folding phone is a thing that opens,
rather than a thing you do something with.

SLIDE 4  What you have that they do not

Four panels. Three hinges. A desk-sized working surface that started the
morning in a pocket.

And an outer layer of ultra-thin glass, which is the part a person touches all
day and the part every review will describe first.

SLIDE 5  The platform line

Open it again.

Three words. It works as a headline, it works as an end frame, it works spoken
over the sound of the hinge, and it survives translation.

SLIDE 6  Why it works

Because it is an instruction, and an instruction is the only kind of headline
that makes a person's hand move.

Every folding device says look at this. Yours says do it again. The second
opening is where the surprise is, and the line puts the audience's own hand on
the surprise instead of a model's hand in a slow motion shot.

Speaker notes: this is the idea. If they buy one thing from this deck, buy
this.

SLIDE 7  Executions, top level

Film. One continuous take. One pair of hands. A desk. No music for the first
eleven seconds.

Print and out of home. The device in the desk state, shot from directly above
at working height, with a real task on it, photographed rather than rendered.

Retail. A demonstration unit on a plinth at exactly the height of a desk, with
nothing on the plinth telling anybody what to do.

SLIDE 8  What we would not do

No slow motion opening shot. No hands from below. No blue gradient background.
No lifestyle footage of somebody using a phone on a mountain.

SLIDE 9  Materials story, if you want one

The outer layer is glass, which is a story about hardness that this category
has told before and which tests well in research.

Our recommendation is that you do not lead with materials at all. Lead with the
second opening, and let the materials answer a question rather than ask one.
