The positioning page holds one argument in one order for everybody. This page
says which part of that argument goes first for each of the six segments, and
which fold state and which finish carries it. The argument does not change. The
entry point does.

The four points, unchanged and in the canonical order:

1. It opens into a work surface.
2. It fits in your pocket when it is shut.
3. The software knows which shape it is in and lays the work out for you.
4. It survives being opened and closed for years.

## The matrix

| Segment | Enter at | Lead state | Lead finish | Channel that carries it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commuter | 2 | phone | Ink, Moss in KR and JP | Retail, carrier, social |
| Desk | 1 | tablet | Chalk | Product page, print |
| Creator | 1 | desk | Moss | Film, out of home |
| Traveller | 2 | tablet | Ink | Airport, carrier |
| Field | 4 | phone | Ink | Long form, reviews, warranty page |
| First-in-line | 1 | desk | Ember | Nothing bought. Owned channels only |

Four of six enter at point 1 or 2. Only Field enters at point 4, and Field is
the only segment where leading on durability does not sound like an apology,
because it is the answer to the question they already asked.

## Segment by segment

### Commuter, 24 percent

Enter at the pocket. Sixty two percent of them expect to use the shut device
most on an ordinary day and every open end mentions one hand, a train or a
crowd. The device has to be worth carrying before it is worth opening.

The mistake to avoid is the full width shot as the first thing a Commuter sees.
It reads as somebody else's product. Show the shut device doing something
useful, then open it once.

Moss leads in Korea and Japan for this segment and everywhere else it is Ink.

### Desk, 21 percent

Enter at the work surface, in the tablet state. This is the segment carrying a
phone and a tablet today and resenting the tablet. The argument writes itself
and the only difficulty is proving it, which means a real document on the
screen at a readable size rather than a photograph of a screen.

One respondent asked to see a spreadsheet with the columns readable. That is
the product page hero and I do not think we should be clever about it.

Chalk is the Desk finish at 31 percent against 23 percent for the base. It is
also the finish that shows the fold line least, which is a coincidence we get
to keep.

**Do we call this segment Desk in a brief that mentions the desk state?**
Not in the same sentence. Desk buyers want the tablet state, and that sentence
goes wrong the moment somebody reads it quickly. Use the full phrase or use the
age band.

### Creator, 18 percent

Enter at the work surface, in the desk state. Forty six percent of everyone who
picked the desk state is in this segment. If the desk state is on film, the
film is talking to Creator, and it should be cast, shot and scored as though it
knows that.

Concentrated in Korea and Japan, thin in Germany and the United Kingdom. Moss
is the only place in the study where a finish beats Ink and it is here.

The line that came out of the open ends and that I keep coming back to: timeline
on the bottom, preview on top, on a train.

### Traveller, 15 percent

Enter at the pocket, land on the tablet state. They read on the device, they
carry two SIMs, and the thing they ask about more than any other segment is how
thick it is shut. Answer that early and in a number.

The most even segment across markets, so one asset with local edits rather than
five.

### Field, 13 percent

Enter at point 4, which we do nowhere else.

Lowest intent of the six at 39 percent. They are not hostile and they are not
vague. They want to know what the device looks like after two years, whether
there is a ridge you can feel with your thumb, and what the warranty covers.

Durability claims for this segment go through Sam before they go anywhere, and
the specific claims we can and cannot make are on their own page. The short
version is that the outer layer changed on 27 August, the crease answer got
better and the scratch answer got worse, and both of those are true and both of
those get said.

Heaviest in Germany, where it is the second largest segment in the market.

### First-in-line, 9 percent

Enter anywhere. They are coming.

Seventy two percent top two box against fifty one for the base, and twenty
seven percent of them pick Ember. Ember buyers overall sit at seventy four
percent intent.

The plan for this segment is to spend nothing on reaching them and to make sure
that when they arrive, Ember is in stock. A finish that is hard to find is a
finish these people do not get to buy. That is a retail allocation
conversation, not a creative one, and it is the only thing I want from this
segment.

## What does not change by segment

- The order of the four points is the order. A segment changes where you enter,
  not what is true.
- No comparison to a named competitor in owned channels, for anyone.
- No material claim without Sam.
- Segment names never leave the building. Fieldhouse has them because they are
  working on the launch. Nobody else does.
- No unit numbers in creative. Forecasts and commitments stay in planning
  documents.
