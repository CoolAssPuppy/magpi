READING THESE TABLES

All counts are unweighted respondents. The base is 1,800 across five markets:
Korea 400, Japan 400, United States 400, Germany 300, United Kingdom 300.

Every crosstab in this volume is exhaustive and mutually exclusive, so every
table totals to 1,800 in both directions. Percentages are rounded to one decimal
and a column may total 99.9 or 100.1 as a result. Cells with a base under 100
are marked with an asterisk and are indicative only.

Index compares a segment's distribution across markets with the distribution of
the whole base. An index of 100 is proportional. Ladderwell treats an index
outside 85 to 115 as worth a client's attention and an index outside 70 to 130
as a finding.

Observations on Table 1.

Creator is a Korea and Japan segment. It indexes 125 in Korea and 114 in Japan

one respondent in four.

Kingdom index of 130 rests on a base of 35 and should not be planned against
without a second reading.

Observations on Table 2.

Creator is the only segment where Moss beats Ink, at 33.3 percent against 32.1

Observations on Table 4.

The desk state is the concept and it is not the expected daily habit. Twenty two
percent of the base named it, against forty one percent for tablet.

Creator is 18.0 percent of the base and 45.9 percent of everyone who named the
desk state. Read the two numbers together before deciding how much of the
creative budget the desk state is worth.

The segment named Desk does not choose the desk state. It chooses the tablet
state at 61.1 percent, the highest single cell in this table. The client's
segment name and the client's fold state name collide here and Ladderwell
recommends the two vocabularies be kept apart in any document a third party
reads.

Commuter at 62.0 percent phone is the second highest cell and is the least
surprising number in the study. Every open end in that segment mentions one
hand, a train or a crowd.

Observations on Table 5.

The two markets with the strongest intent are the two markets where Moss wins.
The client has told us these two also carry the largest share of the first
quarter volume plan. Three separate reasons to weight Korea and Japan point the
same way, and Ladderwell notes that they are not three independent reasons.

Germany is eleven points below Korea and holds the highest Field concentration
in the study. The two facts are the same fact. Field is the lowest intent
segment and Germany has the most of it.

Ember's index of 146 on a base of 200 is the strongest relationship in this
volume and the one most likely to be misread. Choosing Ember does not cause
intent. Ember is the finish that people who already want this device choose.
Making more Ember does not make more buyers.
