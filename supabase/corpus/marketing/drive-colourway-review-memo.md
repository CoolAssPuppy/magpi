# Colourway review, the six finishes and what the panel says about them

Maya Restrepo
2026-08-26
Revised 2026-09-07. Section 6 added after two of the six were cut. Everything
above it stands as written on 26 August, including the part I got wrong.
Related: GTM-18, GTM-31, and the colourway lineup decision page

## What this memo is

Industrial design put six finishes on the bench on 24 August. This is the
marketing read on all six: what the panel says, what each one is for, and which
of them I would defend if we could only keep four.

It is not the decision. The decision page says four ship and hardware picks
which four. This is the input to that.

## 1. What a colourway actually costs

Worth writing down before anyone falls in love with a swatch, because the cost
of a finish is invisible from a bench.

A finish is a coating recipe, a frame process, a folio case tool, a set of
packaging art, a line changeover, and eleven retail SKUs. It is also three
cosmetic units for photography that nobody has built yet, and a support answer
for the six months when one of the four is out of stock everywhere.

None of that scales with how much anyone likes the colour.

## 2. The panel

The panel ran in the week of 17 August, 1,800 respondents across the five tier 1
markets. Korea, Japan, the United States, Germany and the United Kingdom. Six
segments came out of it and the corpus of everything downstream uses these
names.

| Segment | Who | Share of intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commuter | 25 to 34, urban, high screen hours | 24% |
| Desk | 35 to 54, wants the tablet state for documents | 21% |
| Creator | 18 to 29, wants the desk state for editing | 18% |
| Traveller | 30 to 44, dual SIM, frequent flights | 15% |
| Field | 30 to 49, buys on durability | 13% |
| First-in-line | 22 to 40, buys because it is new | 9% |

The single most useful thing the panel produced is negative. Colour does not
move intent on this device. Nobody in 1,800 people said they would buy a folding
phone because of a shade, and the people who said a shade would stop them were
inside the noise.

What colour does move is which one a buyer picks once they have decided, and
that is a stocking question rather than a demand question. It is why the case
for a fifth finish is weak and the case for getting the four right is strong.

## 3. The six, one at a time

**Ink, near black.** The lead. Every render since June is Ink and every press
image will be Ink. It is the least interesting finish on the bench and the most
correct one in a photograph, because a four panel device that opens twice needs
the eye going to the shape rather than to the colour. Flat across all six
segments. First or second everywhere and top by margin nowhere.

**Chalk, warm off-white.** The finding of the review. Under a point source at a
shallow angle the fold line is harder to find on Chalk than on any of the other
five, and it is not close. That is worth more this month than it would have been
in June. It marks, and every off-white phone anyone has owned has gone grey at
the corners, and I would ship it anyway. Strongest with Desk and Traveller.

**Moss, deep green.** Best panel result in Korea and Japan, which between them
are about 40 percent of the first quarter forecast, so it earns a place on that
alone. It also photographs better than anything else on the bench. Every shot of
Moss comes back looking like a coat and every shot of Ink comes back looking
like a phone, and both of those are compliments for different jobs.

**Ember, burnt copper.** Narrow. Bottom three overall and top of the pile among
Creator and First-in-line, both of which over-index on it hard. It is the one
finish where the people who want it really want it, and the one we would carry
least of in every market. Keep it for what it says about the product rather than
for the units.

**Tide, pale blue.** Broadest positive of the six. No strong negative in any of
the five markets, first or second in three of them, and the one the room picked
when I asked for a favourite. It is also the finish I trust least, because the
room was looking at a swatch under a lamp with no coating on it and no frame
around it. Sam will not sign the shade until the outer layer material is
settled, and as of this morning the outer layer material is not settled.

**Rust, oxide red.** The only warm finish that reads as deliberate rather than
as a phone case, which is a real thing to have. It needs a second anodising pass
on the frame. Dana has said twice, to two different people, that a second pass
needs a tooling slot, and we do not have one spare.

## 4. If we could only keep four

Ink, Chalk, Moss and one of Tide or Ember.

Tide is the better commercial answer and Ember is the safer one. If the outer
layer stays on glass I would take Tide and carry a small quantity of Ember for
the people who want it. If the outer layer changes, Tide is the first thing I
would expect to lose, and I would rather say that now than be surprised.

## 5. What I would not do

Add a fifth finish to make a market happy. Give a carrier a finish of its own.
Ship a colour we have only ever seen on a bench. All three of those have been
raised in the last fortnight and all three are the same mistake, which is
treating a finish as a marketing asset rather than as a manufactured part.

## 6. Added 7 September. What happened

Section 4 was right and I would rather it had not been.

The outer layer went from UTG-3 glass to Meniscus-C polymer on 27 August, the
day after this memo. Both of the finishes I named as exposed were cut inside 48
hours of that decision, and neither cut was ours to make.

Tide went first. The polymer hard coat is not optically neutral in the way glass
is, and a pale blue is the one shade on the bench that shows a coating cast.
Under the production coating the blue read yellow-green, and the shift was
larger than anything a print, a swatch or a retail photograph could be matched
to. Hardware measured it rather than eyeballing it, which is the only reason the
conversation took a day instead of a fortnight.

Rust went second, and for a duller reason. The polymer change meant a die
reorder, the die reorder took the tooling slot, and the second anodising pass
had nowhere to go before the pilot line. Not a taste decision and not a colour
problem at all. A calendar.

So the lineup is Ink, Chalk, Moss and Ember. Ember is in because Tide is out,
which is not the argument I made for it in section 3 and is the argument that
ended up mattering.

Two things I want to carry into the next one of these. First, a swatch on a
bench is a swatch on a bench, and I let a room rank six of them under a lamp
knowing that and did it anyway because it is a nice hour. Second, both cuts came
out of a material decision that nobody in this function had any part in, and we
found out about them rather than being asked. That is the correct order. It is
just worth knowing that is the order, because it means the finish work cannot
start until hardware has stopped changing its mind, and hardware stopped
changing its mind eleven weeks before the pilot line.
