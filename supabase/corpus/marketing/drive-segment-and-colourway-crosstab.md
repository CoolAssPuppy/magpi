2026-09-05

Working note, not a decision. Ladderwell's table volume is on its second draft

Maya has the readout covering the whole study. This is the narrower question,
which is what the colour numbers mean for packaging art, for accessories and
for what we ask retail to hold.

## Two findings, and they are not the same kind of finding

**Moss is a market finding.** First choice in Korea at 38.8 percent and in Japan
at 36.3 percent, ahead of Ink in both. Third in the United States, Germany and
the United Kingdom, and by a distance: 19.0, 17.0 and 17.3 percent. That is a
twenty point swing on one finish across five markets and it is the largest
market effect anywhere in the study.

**Ember is a segment finding.** Its share barely moves across markets, from 9.7
percent in Germany to 12.7 percent in the United Kingdom. What moves is who
picks it. Twenty seven percent of First-in-line, 15.7 percent of Creator, 4.8
percent of Desk. Eighteen Desk respondents out of 378 chose it and I would not
quote that as a percentage to anybody.

The distinction matters because the two findings ask for different things. A
market finding is answered by shipping different mixes to different countries.
A segment finding is answered by making sure the finish exists wherever those
people shop, which is not the same problem and is easier to get wrong.

## The mix I would ask for

Not a decision and Dana owns the real one. This is the panel's answer, written
out so there is a starting number to argue with.

| Region | Ink | Chalk | Moss | Ember |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Korea and Japan | 33% | 18% | 37% | 12% |
| United States | 44% | 27% | 17% | 12% |
| Germany and United Kingdom | 44% | 27% | 17% | 12% |

Korea and Japan together are about forty percent of the first quarter plan, so
Moss is not a regional curiosity. It is a large share of a large share.

One caution on my own numbers. Panel first choice was forced. There was no none
of these option, which flatters a minority finish, because somebody who would
never buy any of the four still has to pick one. That should make me want to cut
Ember. It does not, and the reason is the intent crosstab: of the 200 people who
picked Ember, 148 are top two box, a rate of 74.0 percent against 50.7 percent
for the base. The forced choice inflates Ember's headcount and the intent number
says the headcount that is left is real.

So the recommendation on Ember is to hold it at roughly its panel share
everywhere and to fight any conversation that treats twelve percent as a
rounding error. Ladderwell put the warning the other way round in their tables
and it is the right warning: Ember does not cause intent. Ember is what people
who already want this device choose. We are not making buyers by making Ember.
We are failing to serve buyers if we do not.

## Accessories

The folio case comes in all four finishes and the desk stand is Chalk only.

Chalk is the Desk segment's finish, at 31.2 percent against 23.1 percent for the
base, the strongest colour to segment relationship after Ember. Desk is also a
United States segment, indexing 119 there and holding one respondent in four.

Which means the Chalk only desk stand and the segment most likely to want a desk
stand and the market where that segment is heaviest all point at the same place.
I do not think that was planned. It works anyway, and the ask is that the United
States gets the deeper desk stand allocation rather than an even split across
tier 1.

The folio is the opposite case. It is offered in four finishes because people
match it to the device, so folio mix should follow device mix by market and not
have a plan of its own.

## Packaging

The names have to be locked before the art goes to print, which is the one hard
dependency in this note. Four finishes, four names, on a moulded fibre tray with
no plastic, and the tray is not the part that is at risk. The sleeve is, and
Maya has that thread with the printer.

Nothing in the panel touches the names themselves. We never tested them. Ink,
Chalk, Moss and Ember are ours and if anybody asks whether the panel liked the
names, the answer is that the panel saw four renders and no words at all.

## The two finishes that are not in any of this

Two of the six finishes in the August design review were never shown to the
panel, because they were not rendered in time for fieldwork in the week of 17
August. Both were later cut, one for a coating problem and one because there was
no tooling slot, and neither cut was about preference.

I am writing that down because there is a version of this conversation six weeks
from now where somebody says the research killed two colours. The research
never saw them.
