SAMPLE AND METHOD

Online panel, total n = 1,800, five markets. Quotas set on age, gender and
region to the national profile within each market, to a tolerance of plus or
minus three points on each cell. No post-weighting was applied. Counts in this
report and in the table volume are unweighted and sum to the achieved base.

Table 1. Achieved sample by market

  Market                  Base      Field days     Incidence
  Korea                    400      5              41 percent
  Japan                    400      5              38 percent
  United States            400      5              33 percent
  Germany                  300      5              29 percent
  United Kingdom           300      5              31 percent
  Total                  1,800

Screening. Respondents qualified if they personally chose and paid for their
current smartphone within the last thirty six months, and rated themselves 3 or
higher on a five point scale measuring willingness to consider a device in an
unfamiliar form factor. Every respondent in the base of 1,800 passed both
screens. Percentages in this study are therefore percentages of a category
qualified sample and not of the general population.

Concept exposure. Six static renders and one forty second silent animation
showing the device moving through its three fold states. No brand name, no
competitor reference, no price. The client instructed that no price battery be
fielded and none was. Respondents who asked about price in the open ends were
not answered.

Segmentation. K means on a twenty two item attitudinal and behavioural battery,
run on the pooled base. Solutions from four to eight clusters were examined. The
six cluster solution was selected on silhouette width and on the client's
requirement that every cluster be large enough to plan against in a single
market. The six clusters are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

HEADLINE FINDINGS

1. The third fold is the concept. Asked to describe the device in their own
words with the renders removed, 61 percent of the base mentioned a size or a
surface. Twelve percent mentioned folding without mentioning size. The
animation's third state, not its first, is what respondents repeated back.

2. Strong intent is high and should be discounted. Top two box purchase intent
across the base is 50.7 percent. Concept tests without price, availability or a
competitive set routinely overstate. Ladderwell's guidance is to treat the
ranking between segments as sound and the absolute level as a ceiling.

3. Field is the hardest segment and knows what it wants. At 38.9 percent it
sits eleven points below the mean. The open ends are not hostile. They are
specific. Field respondents ask what happens to the device when it is dropped
on the corner, what happens to the fold line after two years, and what the
warranty covers. This is a segment that can be moved by evidence and cannot be
moved by adjectives.

4. Fold state intent splits three ways and not evenly. Forced to name the state
they expect to use most on an ordinary day, 40.9 percent chose tablet, 37.2
percent chose phone and 21.9 percent chose desk. The desk state carries the
concept and is not the state most people expect to live in.

5. Desk state is a Creator behaviour. Creator is 18 percent of the base and 46
percent of everyone who named the desk state. No other segment comes close.

6. Colourway first choice concentrates. Forced to one of four finishes, Ink took
39.2 percent, Moss 26.6 percent, Chalk 23.1 percent and Ember 11.1 percent.

7. Moss is a Korea and Japan result and not a global one. Moss is first choice
in Korea at 38.8 percent and in Japan at 36.3 percent, ahead of Ink in both. In
the United States, Germany and the United Kingdom it runs third. The two markets
where Moss wins are the two markets the client has told us carry the largest
share of the first quarter forecast.

8. Ember is small and it is not weak. Ember is the first choice of 200
respondents, the smallest of the four. Of those 200, 148 are top two box on
intent, a rate of 74.0 percent against 50.7 percent for the base. Ember does not
need to be sold to many people. It needs to reach the people it already lands
with, and a finish that is hard to find in a store is a finish those people do
not buy.

LIMITATIONS

Concept only. Respondents saw renders and an animation. No respondent handled a
device, felt a hinge, or saw a fold line under any real lighting condition. The
crease question in the open ends is a category expectation carried in from other
products and not a reaction to this one.

Four finishes were shown. The client's design review holds six. Two were not
rendered in time for fieldwork and were not exposed. This study says nothing
about them and should not be cited as though it does.

No price was shown. Intent measured without price is not intent.

Sample sizes below 100 are reported in the table volume but are indicative
only. Every cell in the fold state crosstab for First-in-line falls in this
range.
