## The question

Industrial design put six finishes in front of the review on 24 August. The
question is not which six are nice. All six are nice. The question is how many
of them we can actually ship and which ones survive engineering validation.

> **Decision, 26 August.** We ship four finishes and no more. Six go into
> validation. At least two of them die, and hardware picks which two, not
> marketing.

## Why four

Four is not a taste number. It came out of counting the things that multiply.

| Thing that multiplies | Per finish | At four | At six |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Packaging art, sleeve and tray print | one set | four | six |
| Folio case in the matching finish | one tool | four | six |
| Cosmetic units for the shoot | three minimum | twelve | eighteen |
| Factory changeover on the pilot line | one changeover | four | six |
| Retail SKU per market, eleven markets | eleven | forty four | sixty six |

Sixty six SKUs for a first product from a company of seven people is not a
lineup, it is a warehouse problem we would be paying for through all of next
year. Four is the number where every one of those rows stays something a person
can hold in their head.

The second reason is that a fifth and sixth finish do not add demand, they split
it. The panel work says intent moves on the device, not on the shade. A person
who wants a folding phone and cannot have it in pale blue buys it in near black.
A person who does not want a folding phone does not buy one because we made a
pale blue.

## The six

| Finish | Shade | Case for it | Case against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | near black | The lead. Every render we have shot is Ink and every reviewer photograph will be Ink | None. It ships |
| Chalk | warm off-white | Shows the fold line least of the six under a point source. That is not a small thing this month | Marks. Every off-white phone marks |
| Moss | deep green | Best panel result in Korea and Japan, and those two are about 40 percent of the first quarter forecast | Nothing in the panel, but greens are the first thing a retailer cuts an order on |
| Ember | burnt copper | Narrow, and where it lands the intent is the highest of the six. Creator and First-in-line both over-index on it | Narrow means narrow. It is the one we would carry least of |
| Tide | pale blue | Broadest positive across all five tier 1 markets, no strong negative anywhere | Tint stability. Sam has not signed off the shade under the coating and will not until the outer layer is settled |
| Rust | oxide red | The only warm finish that reads as deliberate rather than as a phone case | Second anodising pass on the frame. Dana has been clear that a second pass needs a tooling slot we do not currently have |

## The criteria, in order

Written in order so that when two of these argue, the higher one wins.

1. **Manufacturable inside the current tooling plan.** A finish that needs a
   process step we have not booked is not a finish, it is a proposal.
2. **Stable under the production coating.** Whatever ends up on the outer layer,
   the shade has to be the same shade in a customer's hand as on the swatch.
3. **Does not make the fold line easier to see.** Chalk is here on that ground
   alone.
4. **Distinct at arm's length.** If two of the four read as the same colour in a
   shop, one of them is wasted.
5. **Panel support.** Last, and it is last on purpose. The panel tells us which
   of two viable finishes to prefer. It does not rescue one that cannot be made.

## What could still move this

The outer layer material decision is open as of this morning. B7 has a line
across pane 2 and Sam is working out whether that is one unit or the material.
If Meniscus changes from UTG-3 glass to the Suwon polymer, two of the six are
directly exposed.

- **Tide** is exposed because the polymer hard coat is not optically neutral the
  way glass is, and a pale blue is the shade that shows a coating cast worst.
- **Rust** is exposed because a material change means a tooling reorder, and a
  tooling reorder eats the slot that the second anodising pass would have used.

Neither of those is a marketing call. Both of them are hardware telling us what
we can have. I would rather write that down now than argue about it in
September as though it were a taste question.

**Can we add a fifth finish later, after launch?**
Yes, and that is the intended path for anything cut now. A finish added in the
spring is a story of its own and costs nothing at launch. A finish added to the
launch lineup costs everything at launch.

**Who signs off the final four?**
Jane, on hardware's recommendation. Maya writes the names and the copy.
