outer layer vendor terms, 4 sept 2026. note to self before I say anything in a
meeting

writing this down because I have already been short with dana about it once.
she is running the negotiation. she did not write the contract, and the
contract is the thing I am annoyed about

what we are being asked to accept

net 30. twelve week firm order horizon. shortfall clause invoicing at 100
percent on material committed and not called within 90 days. tooling paid by us
and owned by them. no liability for consequential loss if they are late. no
second site. no contractual buffer stock

read that list again as one sentence. we pay for a fixture we do not own, on a
line we cannot second source, three months before we sell anything, and if they
miss the date we carry the entire cost of a phone that does not ship

the clause I actually cannot get past

clause 7. we pay 214,000 for a die and a slitting fixture and it remains their
property. they undertake to maintain it "for the life of the programme", which
is their definition of programme and their definition of life

if that relationship ever ends badly, the tooling conversation is the
expensive one, and we will be having it from the wrong side of a shipping
container. dana has asked for a separate tooling agreement. whatever pull we
have ends the day the order is placed and the order is three weeks away

what worries me more than clause 7

the twelve week horizon plus the shortfall clause is a cash trap for a company
our size. we commit to material three months out, based on a forecast that is
a work of fiction, and the penalty for being wrong in the low direction is a
100 percent invoice for material sitting in a warehouse in korea

they offered 45 days against a twelve month volume forecast. jane took it. it
is the right trade and I want to be clear with myself that I think it is the
right trade, because I am about to spend a meeting complaining about the
consequence of a decision I agreed with

the asymmetry that actually annoys me
under by 40 percent, we pay a shortfall charge
over by 40 percent, they are under no obligation to supply
so we carry both tails. that is the deal every small customer gets from a big
vendor and knowing it is normal does not make it fine

what a better version looks like
- shortfall charge capped at a percentage of the committed value, not 100
- a supply obligation on the over case, even a soft one, even first refusal
- six weeks of finished stock as an obligation with a number and a remedy in it
- tooling agreement, separate, signed before the purchase order
- price validity longer than six weeks, because "subject to review against the
  base film index" means they can reprice us in november

realistic view: we get one and a half of those five. dana thinks one. she is
usually right and I would like to be wrong once
