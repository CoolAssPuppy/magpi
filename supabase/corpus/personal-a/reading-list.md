# Reading list

Kept because otherwise I buy the same book twice.

## To read

- The Salt Path Home, Ines Marchand. Everyone at book club has read it. I have
  read the first eleven pages four times.
- Weathering, Ada Cornish. Poetry. Bought it in Edinburgh in a shop that had a
  cat.
- A Short History of the Canal Age, R. J. Pelham. Bought this in 2024. Has moved
  house with me once. Still on the list. At this point it is furniture and I have
  made peace with that.
- That long piece about the woman who catalogued moths for forty years. Saved to
  read later, which is where things go to die.
- Notes on Sound, Yusuf Alkan. Someone recommended it and I cannot remember who,
  which is annoying because I want to ask them why.
- The Orchard at Kilmeny, Norah Bright. Dad said it was slow. Dad says everything
  is slow.

## Read

**Small Hours, Delia Anstruther.** Finished it on the train and then sat in the
station for twenty minutes. The ending is not the good part. The forty pages
before the ending are the good part.

**A Field Guide to Rivers, Tom Ellery.** Fine. Too many lists. I say this as a
person who is currently writing a list.

**The Long Wet Spring, Ines Marchand.** Better than the one everybody talks
about. Nobody agrees with me.

**Cold Storage, Kit Vance.** Read it in two days and could not tell you a single
character's name a month later. That is not a complaint exactly.

**The essay about swimming in the reservoir.** Whoever wrote it, I have thought
about it once a week since March. Cannot find it again. Searched the phrase I
remembered and got nothing, which is a familiar feeling.

## Abandoned

- The big Antarctic one. 600 pages and the first 200 are provisioning.
- Something with a lighthouse on the cover. Got 60 pages in twice, a year apart,
  and did not realise until the second time.

## Rules for myself, ignored

- Two on the go, no more.
- Finish the poetry before buying more poetry.
- Do not start anything over 400 pages between October and January.
