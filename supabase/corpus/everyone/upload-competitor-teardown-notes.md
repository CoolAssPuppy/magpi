# Harbourline teardown notes

Elena Vargas
8 May 2026

I ran a trial account on Harbourline for three weeks under my own name
and a made up brokerage. These are my notes as I went, tidied up
slightly. Nothing in here came from a customer or from anyone at
Harbourline. It is all things I clicked on myself.

Posting it here because I have now explained it verbally four times and
each time I forget something.

## What Harbourline is

Same shape as us. Shared workspace for a brokerage, shipment records,
messaging, documents. They came at it from the TMS side rather than the
inbox side and you can feel that in every screen.

Their pitch is "the operating system for your brokerage", which is a
bigger promise than ours and lands well with the operations directors
who buy software rather than the dispatchers who use it.

## What they do well

**Onboarding.** I had a working account with sample data in it in about
eleven minutes without talking to a human. Ours takes a scheduled call
and a mailbox connection. Theirs starts with a spreadsheet import that
guesses your columns and gets most of them right. Their first run
experience is better than ours and it is not close.

**The load board view.** Their main screen is a dense grid, one row per
load, twenty odd columns, everything sortable and inline editable.
Dispatchers who came from a spreadsheet recognise it instantly. Our
thread list is calmer and prettier and it makes an operations manager
who thinks in rows feel like they lost something.

**Carrier records.** They keep a proper carrier profile: insurance
expiry, authority, contacts, lane history, past rates. When you assign a
load they show you what that carrier charged on that lane last time. We
have none of this and I have wanted it for two years.

**Reporting.** Canned reports on margin per load, per customer, per
carrier, per salesperson. Export to spreadsheet from anywhere. Our
reporting is a page with four numbers on it.

**They ship fast.** Two visible changes landed during my three week
trial and both had a note in the app explaining them.

## What they do badly

**Email.** This is the big one. Harbourline treats email as an
attachment to a record rather than as the record. You can log an email
against a load. You cannot work out of Harbourline the way a dispatcher
works out of a mailbox. Their answer is that you should not be working
out of email, which is a fine opinion and does not describe anybody's
Tuesday.

Specifically: no shared inbox, no threading, replies from inside the app
come from a no reply address unless you configure a sending domain, and
the message body renders as plain text with the formatting stripped. I
pasted a rate confirmation into a message and it came out as a wall.

**Documents.** Files attach to the load, not to the conversation, and
there is no preview. Every PDF downloads. On a phone that is painful.

**Density in the wrong places.** The grid is great and everything behind
it is cluttered. The load detail page has six tabs and I could not find
the document list twice in a row.

**Search.** Worth saying, given our own situation, that theirs is not
obviously better. It searches structured fields well, because they have
more structured fields than us, and it does not search message content
at all as far as I could tell.

**Support.** I filed two questions from the trial account. First answer
came in two days, second one never came. Small sample, and I noticed.

## How their pricing is shaped

Published on their site, so this is not a secret.

Three tiers, priced per seat per month, billed annually with a discount
for paying up front. No shipment volume component anywhere. The tiers
are split on features rather than on usage: the cheapest one has no API
and no reporting, the middle one adds both, the top one adds carrier
compliance monitoring and a customer portal.

Implementation fee on the top two tiers, quoted per project. Their sales
page says "typically four to six weeks" for implementation.

Why this matters to us:

- **They are cheaper for a small team that ships a lot.** No overage
means a five person brokerage doing high volume pays the same as a five
person brokerage doing nothing. That is exactly the shape of some of
our best accounts, and it is the argument that beat us at Tolliver.
- **They are much more expensive for a big team that ships little.**
Twenty seats at their rate is a real number and buyers feel it.
- **Annual up front is a barrier we do not have.** Several of our
customers cannot or will not commit for a year. We should stop being
apologetic about monthly.
- **The implementation fee gives us something to say.** We are running
in a fortnight without a project.

## Deals we have lost to them

**Tolliver Logistics.** Lost in February. Nine seats, high volume,
growing. They ran both trials properly and told us honestly why: our
per shipment charge made their bill unpredictable and their finance
person hated it, and Harbourline's carrier profiles solved a problem we
did not touch. The email argument did not move them because their
dispatchers already work in a TMS and have done for years.

I do not think we could have won that one on features. I think we might
have won it on billing predictability if we had had anything to offer
there.

**Cape Anne Forwarding.** Lost in April, and this one annoys me more.
Fourteen seats, moderate volume. They loved the threads. Their
operations director said the word "obviously" about our product twice
in the first demo. We lost it in procurement, on reporting: they wanted
margin per customer per month on day one and we said we would export it
for them, and Harbourline showed them a screen.

Two seats of ours were already using Portside on a trial and were
unhappy about the decision, which I know because one of them emailed me
afterwards.

## What I would take from this

Not a strategy, just what I would argue for.

1. Carrier records with rate history. This came up in both losses and in
two deals we won where the buyer mentioned it as a gap.
2. Something on reporting. Three reports an operations director asks
for in a demo, on a screen, exportable. That is the whole ask.
3. Stop leading with the inbox in demos to buyers who came from a TMS.
Lead with the thread and show the inbox second. When I did it that way
in March the demo went better.
4. We should be able to answer "what will my bill be" better than we do.
That is not a product note exactly, but it lost us Tolliver.
