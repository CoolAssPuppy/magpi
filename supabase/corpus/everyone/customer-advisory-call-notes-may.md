## Finding old threads

This took twenty five minutes of the hour and I did not steer it there.

Ingrid described what she does when she needs a load from two months ago: she
types the carrier name into search, gets a list she cannot narrow, gives up, and
scrolls the thread list by date instead. She has been doing this for over a year
and had never mentioned it to us, because she assumed it was how the product
works. Which, to be fair to her, it is.

Owen said something I want on the record because it is the clearest statement of
the problem I have heard from a customer. He said he does not want better search
results, he wants a smaller list. He knows the load. He knows roughly when. He
knows which customer. He wants to say those three things and see nine threads.

Denise raised the other half of it. She searches for a company name and misses
because of how it is spelled in the thread. Her example was a carrier she has
always typed one way and whose emails come in spelled another way. Hal said
afterwards that this is a large share of what he sees.

Gia's version was about attachments. She remembers a number that was written on a
rate confirmation and there is no way to look for it.

I said we were working on it and did not say what, because we have not decided
what. Nobody pushed.

## An invoice nobody could read

Denise brought a printout, held it up to her camera, and asked us to explain it.

Her account is Standard, $340, five seats, 1,200 shipments included, and she went
over. The invoice showed the plan fee, then a line for shipments at $0.11 each,
and she could not tell from the invoice which month the shipment count covered.
She had assumed the plan fee covered everything and had been passing the invoice
through without reading it for months, so the first time she looked properly she
thought something was wrong.

Nothing was wrong. The charge was correct. Ade confirmed after the call.

The problem is that the line does not say what period the shipments were counted
over, and the trueup runs after the month closes, so the overage on an invoice is
for the month before the one the invoice is dated. That is defensible and it is
not readable.

Hal said he gets a version of this most months. I had not known that.
