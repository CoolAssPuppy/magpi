# Notes from Shipwright 2026

Elena Vargas
3 June 2026

Shipwright, Birmingham, two days. Typed these on the train home so the
back half is worse than the front half.

Short version: worth going, not worth sponsoring, and I would send two
people next year instead of one.

## Sessions I sat in

**Keynote, Tuesday morning.** The organisers talked about what the
conference is for and then a haulier from Hull talked for forty minutes
about succession planning in family firms. Better than it sounds. His
point was that the person who knows how everything works is usually the
one nobody has written anything down about, and when they retire the
firm discovers what was in their head by losing it. Half the room
laughed in the wrong way, which is to say they recognised themselves.

**Carrier onboarding without a compliance team.** Practical. A panel of
three operations people arguing about how much checking is enough before
you give a carrier a load. Two of them do it manually and defend it. The
third has a checklist and a folder and said the checklist took her a
weekend to write and has saved her every week since. I wrote down four
of her items.

**Driver retention.** I went because the room was warm and stayed
because the speaker was good. Everything she said was about scheduling
and predictability rather than pay. Not our problem to solve and it
explains a lot about why dispatchers get shouted at.

**Buying software for a small brokerage.** This was the one I came for
and it was disappointing. Three vendors on a panel, a moderator who did
not push, and forty minutes of nobody saying anything specific. The one
useful moment was an audience question near the end: a woman asked how
you know when you have outgrown a spreadsheet, and none of the three
answered it. I would like a good answer to that question.

**Warehouse automation, afternoon session.** Skipped after ten minutes.
Wrong end of the chain for us.

**Closing panel.** Skipped entirely, went to the bar with two people
from a brokerage in Sheffield.

## Booth conversation

We did not have a booth. I spent an hour at somebody else's.

Long conversation at the stand of a company that does insurance
verification. Not a competitor and not a partner, they check carrier
insurance and authority and sell that as a feed. Their commercial lead
walked me through what they actually send: a nightly file, plus a
webhook when something lapses.

The interesting part was how many people stopped at their stand to
complain rather than to buy. Three separate visitors while I was
standing there described the same problem, which is that they find out
a carrier's insurance has lapsed after the load has moved.

I asked whether anyone ever asks them to put that in front of a
dispatcher rather than in a file, and he said constantly, and that they
have no interest in building a screen for it. That is worth a
conversation with product at some point. Filing it here rather than
starting one now.

## Person I need to find

Second evening, at the drinks, I talked for twenty minutes to a man who
runs operations for a mid sized brokerage somewhere in the north west.
Grey jacket, had come straight from a customer visit, drinks orange
juice. He described exactly the succession problem from the keynote,
except in his case it is his own operations manager who is retiring in
January and who is the only person who knows the history on their
forty biggest customers.

He asked me two very sharp questions about how you get somebody's
working knowledge out of their head and into a place the next person can
read.

I did not write his name down. I have his face and his firm's approximate
size and nothing else. I did not take a card because I did not have any
of mine and I felt awkward about it, which is a stupid reason.

Things I know: north west, mid sized, operations manager retiring in
January, he mentioned a depot near Warrington, and he said he had been
to Shipwright four years running. The organisers publish an attendee
list to exhibitors and we were not an exhibitor. Someone at the
insurance stand might know him.

If anyone reading this recognises that description, tell me.

## Practical notes for next year

- The venue is fine, the wifi is not. Tether.
- Sessions run late from mid morning onwards and nobody adjusts the
schedule, so the last session of the day starts about twenty minutes
after it says it will.
- The conference app is worse than the paper programme. Take the paper
one from the desk on the way in, they run out.
- Badges do not show company size or role, which makes the drinks
harder work than it needs to be.
- Two people is the right number. One person cannot be in the corridor
and in a session at the same time and the corridor is where the
conversations happen.
- Sponsoring: the sponsor stands were quiet all Tuesday and busy for
about ninety minutes on Wednesday morning. I would not pay for that.

## Where to eat

There is a small Kashmiri place about ten minutes walk from the venue,
up past the car park and left. No sign to speak of, green door, the menu
is on a board. Best meal I have had at a work thing. They do a lamb
karahi that four of us shared and then ordered again.

Do not go to the hotel restaurant. Everyone goes to the hotel restaurant
and it took ninety minutes to get a starter.
