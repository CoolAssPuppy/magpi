# Outer layer supply, quote analysis and what the terms are worth

Dana Provenzano
2026-09-04
Related: OPS-47, OPS-43, HW-138, and quotation SAF-Q-26-2288

## The question

We have one quote for the material we just committed the whole product to, and
two candidate second sources at the sample stage. This is what the three of
them cost, what the terms are worth in cash, and what I recommend.

The short version: the incumbent quote is fair on price and poor on terms, the
cheaper candidate cannot be qualified in time to matter this year, and the
expensive candidate is the one worth paying for, later.

## Price

Per finished device, converted film delivered to the pilot line.

| Price at 250,000 units | $44.80 | $42.10 | $50.60 |

At 50,000 units the spread between the cheapest and the dearest is $10.50 a
device, which is $525,000 across fifty thousand units. That is a real number
and it is the wrong number to decide this on.

## What the terms are worth

Price per piece is the part everyone looks at. The terms are where the money
actually moves.

**Payment terms.** Thirty days net against sixty days net, on roughly $2.8M of
material a year at the first volume break, is about $230,000 of working capital
we have to find. John can put a cost of capital on that better than I can, and
whatever the number is, we are a seven person company and the cash matters more
to us than the interest does.

**Firm order horizon.** Twelve weeks means we commit to material three months
before we sell a device. Combined with the shortfall clause, which invoices at
100 percent for material committed and not called within 90 days, that horizon
is the single largest cash risk in my part of the business. Candidate 2's eight
week horizon is worth more to us than their price premium costs.

**The expedite waiver.** The incumbent waives $18,800 of air freight charges on
the first two shipments if the firm order is placed before 30 September. That
is a genuine deadline with a genuine number attached and it is four weeks away.

**Tooling ownership.** We pay $214,000 for a fixture that stays their property
under clause 7 of their standard terms. Every vendor writes this clause and
most of them will sign a separate tooling agreement if asked. Nobody has asked.
I am asking.

**Supply liability.** Clause 9 excludes consequential loss. If they are six
weeks late we carry the entire cost of a device that does not ship. This is
normal, it is also the reason buffer stock has to be a contractual obligation
and not a favour, and it is the thing I have been pushing on since 2 September.

## The second source question

Candidate 1 is cheaper by $3.10 a device and needs about ten weeks from sample
to approved. Samples arrive the week of 14 September and the cycle rig is
booked for them the week after. Ten weeks from there is a 2027 answer.

Candidate 2 is dearer by $7.40 a device, has two sites producing the film, and
offers sixty day terms and an eight week horizon. On paper that is the better
supplier and we cannot use it this year either.

So the second source is not a launch answer under any arrangement, and I want
that written down clearly rather than left as an open item that quietly reads
like a plan. Between now and a qualified second source, our exposure is one
line, one building, one typhoon season. The incumbent holds roughly six weeks
of finished stock for us once volume starts. Past six weeks of an outage, we
stop shipping.

## What I recommend

1. Place the firm order with the incumbent before 30 September and take the
   $18,800 expedite waiver.
2. Ask for a separate tooling agreement on the $214,000 fixture before that
   order goes in, because we have the strongest hand in the four weeks before we
   place it and none at all the day after.
3. Take the 45 day payment terms they have offered against a twelve month
   forecast, and negotiate hard on the over-forecast case rather than the
   under-forecast one. Being unable to supply a phone that is selling is a
   worse outcome for us than a shortfall charge.
4. Get six weeks of finished stock written into the supply agreement as an
   obligation, with a number and a remedy.
5. Continue qualifying candidate 2 and treat candidate 1 as a price reference
   we can show the incumbent at the next review.

## What I need

A decision on point 1 by 20 September, because the order takes a week to
assemble and I do not want to find out on the 29th that somebody wants to
re-read the contract.
