# First quarter revenue model, 180,000 units

John Mbeki
2026-09-09

This is the model behind the number Jane has been quoting in board conversations
since the end of August. It has been three different spreadsheets and one
argument, and this memo is the version I am willing to be held to.

Read the caveat in the last section before you quote anything out of the middle
of it.

## The one line

180,000 units in the first quarter, across tier 1 and tier 2, at $1,899, is
$341,820,000 of device revenue and $136,620,000 of gross margin.

Everything else here is that sentence taken apart.

## Where 180,000 comes from

It is not a bottom-up build of nine markets. It is a top-down number that two
independent methods bracketed and a third constrained, which is the only kind of
forecast I trust for a product nobody has ever bought.

The first method is the carrier indications. Two carriers, A and B, gave sell-in
ranges. The commitment numbers live with marketing and I have modelled against
the bottom of both ranges, because a carrier range is a negotiating position
until it is a purchase order.

The second is the panel. 1,800 respondents across the five tier 1 markets in the
week of 17 August, converted at an intent-to-purchase rate I took from two
published foldable launches rather than from our own optimism.

The two came in at 164,000 and 191,000, and the midpoint is 177,500.

The third thing I did was check the ceiling. Suwon holds 8 coating runs at
26,000 pieces each, which is 208,000 pieces, which is 200,000 devices after the
4 percent lamination scrap allowance. The pilot line and the anodising slots
agree with that number. 180,000 is the midpoint rounded up to something a person
can say out loud, and it uses 90 percent of the capacity we hold.

The mix is the softest assumption in this memo. It came from the panel and the
panel asked people to pick a colour from a render, which is not the same
question as picking one in a shop with a two week wait on the one you wanted.

Ember at 13.0 percent is the number I would bet against in either direction. It
out-performs everything else in two segments and does nothing anywhere else, and
a narrow finish is either 9 percent or 17 percent and very rarely 13. Ember is
also the finish with one coating run and no second one inside six weeks, so
being wrong upwards and being wrong downwards cost us different things and only
one of them is recoverable.

## What happens if the volume is wrong

Unit cost does not move with volume in this model. The pilot line rate is in
there at every point on the curve, which is deliberately conservative, so gross
margin percent is flat across the whole table.

| Units | Revenue | Gross margin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 140,000 | $265,860,000 | $106,260,000 |
| 160,000 | $303,840,000 | $121,440,000 |
| 180,000 | $341,820,000 | $136,620,000 |
| 200,000 | $379,800,000 | $151,800,000 |
| 220,000 | $417,780,000 | $166,980,000 |

220,000 is in the table for completeness and it cannot happen in this quarter.
The coating capacity we hold is 200,000 devices and the next slot after that is
six weeks out, so anything above 200,000 is a second quarter number wearing a
first quarter hat.

If we get to the volume assembly rate instead of the pilot rate, unit cost drops
to $1,104 and margin per unit goes to $795. At 200,000 units that is
$159,000,000 instead of $151,800,000. I have left it out of the headline because
the pilot line is booked for a pilot line and the transition to volume rate is a
decision nobody has taken.

## The part that keeps me awake

The revenue model works. The cash model is a different document and it is worse.

Carrier sell-in moves title on 71,000 units, which is A at 46,000 across Korea,
the United States, Germany and Canada, and B at 25,000 across Japan, the United
Kingdom, Australia and France. That leaves 109,000 units, or $124,260,000 at
cost, that we build, hold and fund ourselves until somebody buys them one at a
time.

The Netherlands is the clearest version of the problem and the smallest. 5,000
units, no carrier, so every one of those devices is ours from the coating run
until a person walks out of a shop with it. It is 2.8 percent of the quarter and
it is 100 percent self-funded, and nobody has told me who is closing that gap or
whether it is being closed at all.

On the phasing in Dana's build plan, peak inventory funded by us is around
46,000 units, or $52,440,000. That is the number that decides whether 180,000 is
a plan or a sentence in a deck. A seven person company does not fund $52,440,000
of inventory out of the bank account, so between now and the first purchase
order we either have an inventory facility, a deposit structure with the pilot
line, or a smaller first quarter.

I would rather write that down now than explain it in December.

## What I would change if I were wrong

Three things would move this model enough to rewrite it.

A lamination yield at 92 percent, because that puts unit cost at $1,159 and
gross margin at 39 percent, and anything below that keeps going. Dana's caveat
on the first article build is the live version of that risk.

A carrier commitment at the bottom of the range in both markets rather than one,
which takes tier 1 towards 130,000 and the quarter towards 160,000.

A mix error on Moss, which is the finish that depends most on two markets. Moss
is 40,195 units and 22.3 percent of the plan, 34,840 of those units are tier 1,
and 21,700 of those are Korea and Japan. Sixty two percent of every green phone
we build in the quarter goes to two markets. If the panel result does not
survive contact with a shop floor, that is the finish that goes long.
