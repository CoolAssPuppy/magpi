# Why the Fold S1 is $1,899

Jane Okonkwo
2026-09-04
Related: OPS-40

## The decision

The Fold S1 launches at $1,899.

I made this call today, on John's model, after two weeks of arguing about it in
a Linear thread that three of us could see. Writing it down here because a price
decided in a comment thread is a price nobody can find in November, and because
the reasoning matters more than the number to anyone who has to defend it.

## What it rests on

Unit cost at pilot volume is $1,140. That number closed on 2 September and the
line by line walkthrough is in this space. At $1,899 we hold roughly 40 percent
gross margin before channel costs.

Forty percent is survivable for a first generation device built on a pilot line
at pilot line costs, and it is well short of what a mature hardware business
runs on. It improves on its own as volume moves assembly labour and scrap in
the right direction without anybody being clever.

## The three prices we considered

**$1,799.** The most defensible price against a spec sheet and the one the
product deserves on the merits. It takes gross margin to about 37 percent
before channel, and below $1,799 the arithmetic on our channel arrangements
stops working from our side. It also sets a ceiling we would have to live under
for the second generation, and the second generation costs more to build than
this one does, because it will have a better hinge.

**$1,899.** Forty percent before channel. Enough room for a promotional period
without the programme going underwater. Close enough to $1,799 that nobody
writes the "surprisingly expensive" paragraph, and far enough from $1,999 that
we are not asking a first generation product to win a price perception fight
against established flagships.

**$1,999.** The number a spreadsheet picks. It buys about $100 of margin per
device and costs us the first sentence of every review. A four digit price
starting with a two is a different conversation from one starting with a one,
and we would be having that conversation about a device with a visible fold
line and a first generation hinge.

## What I am actually buying with the $100 I gave up

Room to be wrong about the crease.

Every reviewer will find the fold line. We have measured it, we know what it
looks like, and we are going to hand them the photograph ourselves. At $1,899
that is a fair trade a reader can make for themselves. At $1,999 it becomes
evidence in a complaint.

I am also buying room to be wrong about the yield. If the lamination yield
comes back at 80 percent the unit cost goes to about $1,154 and the margin
narrows by a point. At $1,999 I would have spent that headroom before we
measured it.

## What would change this

1. Unit cost moves more than $60 in either direction. Freight is the volatile
   line, and it can move $33 on its own if the schedule ever gets enough slack
   to put the devices on a ship.
2. The pilot build gives us a lamination yield materially worse than 80 percent.
3. A competitor prices a comparable device somewhere that makes $1,899 look
   either greedy or cheap. Cheap is the one that would worry me.

Anything else, including a strong opinion from somebody who has just read one
analyst note, is not a reason to reopen this.

## Who holds this number

Finance holds it. John, Dana and me.

It stays inside this space until I say otherwise, and I will say otherwise on
the day the marketing group needs it to build the pages that carry it. Nobody
outside Finance needs to know either the cost or the price to do their job
today, and the two numbers together tell a stranger more about this company
than I want a stranger to have.

A price before it is announced belongs to the smallest number of people who
can still do their jobs, and that has nothing to do with trust.
