## The chain

Four numbers decide the tooling order, and they decide it in this order.

1. The first quarter forecast is 180,000 units across tier 1 and tier 2, which
   is 148,500 and 31,500.
2. Korea and Japan are 72,000 units, exactly 40.0 percent.
3. Four finishes ship. Ink 68,245, Chalk 48,125, Moss 40,195, Ember 23,435.
4. Every finish needs its own coating run at Suwon and its own pass on the
   anodising line, so the count of finishes is a capex line and a schedule line
   at the same time.

Nobody picked four colours to save money. The review on 24 August cut Tide for a
tint problem under the Meniscus-C coating and cut Rust because a second
anodising pass had nowhere to go. Both cuts trace back to the polymer reversal
on 27 August. What this page does is write down what those two cuts were worth,
because the number turned out to be larger than anyone in the review expected.

## What the tooling order changed

The order Dana was holding on 31 August was written for six finishes. This is
the same order at four.

| Line | Per finish | Six finishes | Four finishes | Delta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suwon tint recipe qualification | $11,800 | $70,800 | $47,200 | -$23,600 |
| Anodising rack set | $26,000 | $156,000 | $104,000 | -$52,000 |
| Cosmetic spec re-measurement | $4,200 | $25,200 | $16,800 | -$8,400 |
| Packaging art plates | $3,100 | $18,600 | $12,400 | -$6,200 |
| Folio case tooling | $19,500 | $117,000 | $78,000 | -$39,000 |
| **Total** | **$64,600** | **$387,600** | **$258,400** | **-$129,200** |

$129,200 of capex we do not spend, on a decision that was taken on cosmetic and
schedule grounds and never came to me for a number. I am not complaining about
the outcome. I am noting that a $129,200 capex decision was made in a design
review and finance found out afterwards.

The die itself does not move. TOOL-SP004 is $214,000 whatever the colour is,
because colour is a coating recipe rather than a cut. That is the reason the
tooling saving is smaller than the panic in the room on 31 August suggested it
would be.

## What it does to unit cost

Changeover cost is inside the shell and frame line and the outer layer line of
the cost model. It is not a new line on top of $1,140.

| | Six finishes | Four finishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coating runs | 10 | 8 |
| Anodising passes | 11 | 8 |
| Setup, total | $484,120 | $360,000 |
| Setup per unit | $2.69 | $2.00 |
| Pigment per unit | $0.48 | $0.48 |
| Colour cost per unit | $3.17 | $2.48 |

A changeover is $34,000 at Suwon, which is the line purge, the tint
requalification against the cosmetic spec and the scrapped lead-in, plus $11,000
at the shell vendor for the anodising rack swap. Call it $45,000 to start a
colour.

Pigment is the same $0.48 a unit on both plans, because pigment follows units
and units do not change. The whole 69 cent difference is setup, which is a way
of saying that a colour costs money to start and almost nothing to continue.

Sixty nine cents a unit is inside the noise of a $1,140 model and I would not
have reopened revision 7 for it. It matters because of what it says about the
sixth colour rather than what it does to the total. Rust at 6,800 units needed
$45,000 to start the colour and another $34,120 for the second anodising pass,
which is $79,120 of setup against $12,913,200 of revenue, and every one of those
6,800 units would have gone through the anodising line twice.

## Inventory is the real argument

| Risk | Four finishes | Six finishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smallest finish, units | 23,435 | 6,800 |
| Mix error of 5 points, units | 9,000 | 9,000 |
| Wrong-colour stock at cost | $10,260,000 | $10,260,000 |
| Finishes below the 5,000 folio minimum | none | Rust |

A mix error costs the same in units whether there are four finishes or six. What
changes is how likely it is. Four buckets are easier to forecast than six, the
smallest bucket is three and a half times larger, and a finish at 23,435 units
can absorb a bad month in a way a finish at 6,800 cannot.

There is a second thing four buckets buy us and I did not see it until Dana ran
the film order. Ember at 24,372 pieces is 628 pieces short of the 25,000 price
band at Suwon, so ordering slightly more film costs a quarter of a million
dollars less than ordering slightly less. That move only exists because Ember is
close to a band edge. At six finishes Ember was 7,424 pieces short and there was
nothing to reach for. Dana has the whole thing in her supply memo.

**Why does the folio minimum matter?**
The case vendor will not run under 5,000 pieces. Rust would have come in at
about 2,300 folios, so either we buy 5,000 and write off half, or the sixth
colourway ships without a case in its own finish. Both answers are worse than
not having the colour.
