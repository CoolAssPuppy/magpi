## Terms that matter

**Suwon, net 30 today and a 12 week firm order horizon.** Their other customers
order in millions and their standard terms reflect that. Thirty days net with a
twelve week horizon means committing cash to material three months before we
sell a phone. I asked for 60 days and they will discuss 45 against a twelve
month volume forecast. Jane took that on 8 September. The redline goes out this
week and it also asks for six weeks of finished stock held at the vendor and
first refusal on capacity above the forecast. I expect to get one of the two.

The 5,000 panel minimum is waived for the first lot and it comes back after
that. A minimum that size against a pilot build means one lot decision covers
the whole run, so a bad lot is not a partial problem.

**Hinge supplier, net 30 and no second source.** This is the exposure in the
whole vendor list. They are the only people making our cam profile, the tooling
is theirs to schedule, and the eight week lead time means a problem found on a
Monday is a problem for two months.

**Display module vendor, net 60.** The best terms we have and the longest lead
time. The two are related and we accepted it.

> Tooling paid to a vendor sits on their floor. The shell tooling, the hinge
> tooling and the polymer die are all in that category. If a relationship ends
> badly, the tooling conversation is the expensive one. Legal has the clauses.

**Why did the glass cancellation cost nothing?**
The sample lot request had not been converted to a purchase order. That was
luck and eight days of margin on a paperwork step, and I would not count on it
twice.

**Who talks to vendors?**
Dana. Engineering asks Dana. A direct conversation between an engineer and a
vendor about price or schedule is how a supplier learns which of us to call when
they want an answer they like.
