## The six weeks are worth more than they look

Tier 2 stock does not have to be built on the tier 1 mix. It has to be built on
what tier 1 actually sold.

Six weeks between the two launches is enough time to see real orders in five
markets and re-cut the tier 2 colour mix before the second build goes down. It
is not enough time to change the total, because the film has a six week lead and
a twelve week firm order horizon. What it changes is which finish 31,500 units
come out as.

| | Units exposed |
| --- | --- |
| Total quarter | 180,000 |
| Committed on the forecast mix, tier 1 | 148,500 |
| Re-cuttable on real orders, tier 2 | 31,500 |

A mix error costs us on 148,500 units and not on 180,000, provided we actually
do the re-cut rather than letting the second build inherit the first one's
spreadsheet. That is a calendar item and it belongs to me.

Tier 2 is 17.5 percent of the quarter, which is a smaller correction window
than I would like. Five of the nine markets and 82.5 percent of the units are
committed on a forecast built from renders, and the re-cut reaches the other
17.5 percent. The forecast has to be good, not just correctable.

**When does the re-cut have to happen?**
Three weeks after tier 1 goes on sale. The film order for the tier 2 build has
to be firm six weeks before the pilot line needs it, so three weeks of real
orders is what we get.

**What if the mix moves more than we can absorb?**
Moss is the release valve. Two Moss runs cover 41,803 pieces against 52,000 of
run capacity, so there are 10,197 pieces of unused run sitting in the Moss slots
and only 1,950 in the Chalk ones. Ember cannot do that job. There is one Ember
run and there is no second one inside six weeks, which is why the Ember run is
being filled to 25,000 pieces rather than to the 24,372 the forecast calls for.

## France and Australia are not the same launch

This is the problem nobody has picked up and I am picking it up here.

Tier 2 is one date for all four markets. Six weeks after a November launch puts
it in the middle of December, and December is not the same month in Rotterdam
and in Sydney.

France opens into the last days of a gifting season. Stock that is not on a
shelf on day one is not late, it is stranded, because the next real selling
window in France is spring. All 9,500 French units have to be in Rotterdam
before tier 2 opens or a large part of them do not move for a quarter.

Australia opens into the front of summer and its biggest retail run of the year,
which goes through Boxing Day and well into January. Australian demand is
back-weighted by design. The 8,000 units still have to be built in the same
pre-launch window, because a January coating run delivers in February, so what
changes is not when we make them but how long they sit in Sydney before somebody
buys one.

| | France | Australia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Units | 9,500 | 8,000 |
| Value at cost | $10,830,000 | $9,120,000 |
| Demand shape | day one or spring | back-weighted into January |
| What we are exposed to | stranded stock | six extra weeks of holding |
| Roughly what that is worth | $10,830,000 at risk | $94,700 of carry |

Two orders of magnitude between the two, on markets 1,500 units apart. So the
rule is that France is served first and served complete, and Australia can wait,
and I would rather short Sydney by a week than short Rotterdam by a box.

The Netherlands sits under this as well and for a different reason. 5,000 units
and no carrier, so there is no sell-in to move title and every one of those
devices is on our books until a person buys it. It is the smallest market in the
plan and the only one that is entirely ours to fund. Nobody has told me who is
closing that gap.
