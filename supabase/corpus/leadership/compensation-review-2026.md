# Compensation review 2026

Owner: Diane Ockley
Date: 22 July 2026
Status: decided, apart from the January item
Circulation: this space only. Marcus and me

This page names three people and says what they earn. It does not leave this
space, it does not get pasted into a message, and it does not get read over a
shoulder in the Manchester office. If you need to discuss any of it with a
manager, discuss the person in front of you and nothing else on the page.

## The process, unchanged from last year

One cycle a year. Effective 1 September, paid in the September payroll.

- Every manager writes half a page per person: what changed in their scope this
  year, what they are visibly better at than twelve months ago, and a
  recommendation. No form, no self assessment, no rating.
- Marcus and I read all 31 in one afternoon. Reading them together is the point,
  because a strong page from a generous manager and a flat page from a terse one
  look different and mean the same thing.
- No curve, no forced distribution, no quota of top performers. A curve on 31
  people is a way of pretending a decision is arithmetic.
- Managers deliver the outcome in a one to one, in person or on video. Nobody
  finds out from a payslip and nobody finds out by email.

Two things we get asked about every year. We do not tie compensation to a
promotion cycle, because we do not have one. And we do not negotiate after the
conversation, because the person who negotiates hardest is not the person who
was underpaid.

He has been below the floor since the bands were set, because he was hired into

## Market adjustment: not this year

Marcus argued for a company wide market adjustment on top of the normal cycle,
on the grounds that the bands have not moved since January 2025 and that
everything we buy has. That is a fair argument and I have said no to it.

The reasons, in order:

1. We change our prices in October and existing customers move at their
   renewals, so we do not know what the change is worth until the renewals run
   through Q1. Committing permanent payroll in September against revenue I will
   not have seen until February is the kind of decision a company that has
   raised money makes. We have not raised money.
2. Five new people arrive between September and January at current bands. Those
   five offers are the best market data we are going to get and they arrive
   before January.
3. Doing it properly means moving the whole band structure. I would rather do
   that once, in January, with the offer data, than apply a percentage across
   the board and call it a review.

**What happens in January.** Marcus and I re-baseline every band using what we
actually had to pay for the five H2 hires. If the bands are wrong, they move,
and the correction is backdated to 1 January rather than applied from whenever
we finish arguing about it. That backdating is the commitment and it is the part
Marcus asked for in writing.

If we come out of the October renewals materially better than plan, this comes
forward and does not wait for January.
